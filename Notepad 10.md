Here are my ideas for the form of spells:

Spells are three things:
1. Spells are instructions. Each step must be: clear, well-defined, specific, purposive, actionable, and complete. This is **why** Visualization is such a crucial step in Spell Casting. You must envision the instructions you want to program into physical reality.
2. Internally, spells are a sequence of memories, thoughts, and actions (in the case of gestures, incantations, and chants—all three could be implemented as choreography for longer rituals).
3. Externally, spells are written down into patterns theough either script or shape. How this looks like depends on the civilization . Obviously magic written in English would be written in the Enflish language while magic written in Chinese would use Chinese, French in French, and so on. You can also draw them! After all, illustrating instructions and representing them graphically is valid. Aliens would use their own representation system. 
	- If you are psionic, you can extract memories and thoughts directly from the mind—which is what Battra built his Tree of Magic around on. Thus, being Psionic helps—but being both Unnon and Unnul is SO rare it's not worth to spec into it. For all of Battra's inferiority complex towards his twin sister, he's lucky to be both Psychic and Sorcerer. 
	- Being Psychic enables you to possess a better brain, which means you have better memory, better visualization, and all the benefits a more efficient and powerful brain gives.
	- But you also have to tame both systems and iron out any weird interactions that might occur by mixing both, so I suppose Battra is still vindicated in his grievances. From his perspective he had to work twice as hard or even four times as hard. Sorcery is already a demanding discipline, layering psionics on top of it introduced more complexity!

It's important to note that how you store a spell is entirely dependent on who you are, due to the notion of the Soul. Magic is personable, it reflects your identity. 

Just like we can arrange the alphabet in any manner or draw random things on paper, we can express magic in much the same way. But, it won't make sense to OTHER people. That's why we have code syntax and best practices, no?Therefore, there have been schools of thought that seek to standardize certain practices. You may think Battra is a proponent for this but he actually encourages his students to do the opposite and express their magic their own way! 

It's certainly tougher, and uncharacteristic of him, but unlike any other normal teacher, Battra is immortal—he's got all the time to teach, he has nothing to lose by waiting for his students to develop. The onus is on the students to grow quickly, or not—he's fine either way. 

Plus, it touches on the fundamental fact of what magic IS: an expression of the Soul. And, if you read Rigorous Magic, you would know that Battra preaches fundamentals a lot. You can't optimize what you don't understand, after all—and nobody knows this better than the being with the 0.000001% Excess rate.

I'm pleased with this framework, but the challenge is feasibly implementing them with the Spell Casting Paradigms (SCP):
- Traditionals can be expressed, for example, either internally as choreography or externally as a recipe-book.
- Conditionals is easy to represent externally, but somehow difficult to express internally. Perhaps 
- Techniques are challenging to visualize storing internally rather than externally. But, to break it down, we can take the Singularity Technique as an example. Convergence and Coordination are two different spells, each with their own instructions, and thus their visualizations, own gestures, own incantations, and own chants. Under Singularity, you can have new methods spawned from the interaction of both individual spells.
