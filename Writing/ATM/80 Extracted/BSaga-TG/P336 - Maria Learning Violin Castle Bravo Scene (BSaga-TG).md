# Maria Learning Violin Castle Bravo Scene
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - Branch of The Saga of the _Titanus gojira_.md` — Prompt 336

## Your Notes

Oh, I'd LOVE to see that scene of Maria being introduced to the violin and "learning" how to play it come alive. The timeline is 2022, so no Battra, but there are students, Madison, and a watching Godric.

## Analysis

**(Setting: The Castle Bravo Observation Lounge. Late afternoon light filters through several hundred feet of Pacific seawater, casting a tranquil, undulating blue across the room. Several of the psychic students—Stella and Suki among them—are huddled on a sofa, studying. In his customary corner, Godric sits like a statue of basalt, a Serizawa notebook open on his lap, though his golden eyes, currently veiled in vigilant blue, are fixed on the room at large. Madison is slumped in a beanbag chair, her thumb rhythmically scrolling through a phone.)**

The quiet was broken by the sharp, melodic trill of a violin emanating from Madison’s speakers. On the screen, a human prodigy’s fingers blurred across the strings, delivering the frantic, joyful complexity of Mendelssohn.

Maria, who had been across the room meticulously organizing a stack of historical texts, stilled. She didn't walk toward Madison; she seemed to drift, her presence arriving before her physical form did. She leaned over Madison’s shoulder, her sapphire eyes widening as she watched the bow dance.

"What is this... 'machine'?" Maria whispered. Her voice carried that curious, melodic lilt that always made the students look up from their homework.

"It's a violin, Maria," Madison said, tilting the screen up. "Four strings, some wood, and a whole lot of soul. It’s pretty much the hardest thing a human can learn to do with their hands."

Maria watched for a full minute, her pupils dilating as she tracked the motion of the bow. To her, it wasn't just movement; it was a frequency. A pattern of resonance that mirrored the very vibrations of the *Psi Force*.

"It is beautiful," Maria murmured. "It sounds like... the way the air feels just before a storm."

Godric shifted in his chair. The slight creak of the leather was the only sign he was listening. He looked at the instrument on the screen, then at the graceful, slender hands of his Queen. He thought of the raw power in those hands—hands that could crush stone—and the idea of them touching something so fragile was a contradiction he couldn't quite resolve.

**One Week Later**

Madison walked into the lounge carrying a rectangular case of dark, polished wood. She looked like she was delivering a holy relic. Behind her, Mark Russell followed, rubbing his temples with the look of a man who was already drafting a very difficult email to the board of directors.

"I got it," Madison announced. "A French luthier’s model. Professional grade. Don't ask how much it cost, Dad’s already crying."

Maria rose from her seat, her movements characterized by an ancient, royal poise. She approached the case as if it were a dormant Titan. Madison clicked the latches open.

The violin rested in its velvet bed, a deep amber-gold. Maria’s breath hitched. She reached out, her fingers hovering inches above the wood.

"It is tangible," Maria said softly, almost to herself. "So much sound hidden in such a small, tangible thing."

She picked it up. Her grip was wrong, the bow held like a stick, but she felt the weight. She looked at Madison. "How do I make it... speak?"

Madison pulled out a stand and some sheet music. "Okay, first, the symbols. This is a clef. These lines are the pitch. These dots are the duration. It’s basically a code for your hands."

For the next twenty minutes, the lounge was silent as Madison gave a crash course in music theory. The students watched, fascinated. Godric had abandoned his book entirely, his arms crossed over his chest, his gaze intense. He was watching the "Learning Engine" of the Divine Moth at work.

Maria didn't ask "why." She didn't struggle with the logic. Her mind, capable of coordinating the global Telepathic Web, absorbed the "code" of the sheet music as if it were a simple dialect.

"I see," Maria said, her voice dropping into a tone of absolute clarity. "The bridge is the anchor. The string is the will. The bow is the breath."

She tucked the violin under her chin. It looked natural there, as if her human form had been designed specifically to hold it. She looked at the sheet music: *Bach’s Sonata No. 3, Allegro Assai.*

"Ready?" Madison asked, her hand on her phone's record button.

Maria didn't answer. She simply closed her eyes.

The first note didn't just play; it **ignited**.

The room was suddenly flooded with a sound so pure and so technical that Mark Russell dropped his tablet. It wasn't the tentative scratching of a beginner. It was the *Allegro Assai* delivered at the speed of thought. 

The students gasped as the music took on a physical quality. Because Maria was a psychic, she wasn't just playing notes; she was projecting the *intent* of the music. The lounge didn't just hear Bach; they felt the rush of a mountain stream, the exhilaration of flight, the crystalline perfection of a star.

Her fingers moved with a precision that defied human biology. There was no hesitation, no "learning curve." She simply took the blueprint Madison had given her and realized it into the world.

In the corner, Godric’s blue eyes flickered. For a split second, they turned a deep, molten gold. He felt the vibration of the music in his own marrow—the "Gravel" of his soul responding to the "Velvet" of hers. He saw the way she looked—the light of the base catching the platinum of her hair, the sheer, unbridled joy on her face as she mastered this human art. 

It was the most beautiful thing he had ever seen. 

Maria finished with a final, soaring note that seemed to linger in the water outside the glass. Silence returned to the lounge, heavy and stunned.

Maria lowered the violin, her chest heaving slightly, a brilliant smile breaking across her face. She looked at Madison.

"I think," Maria said, her eyes shimmering with sapphire light, "I should like to try the piano next."

Madison stared at her, then turned to her father. "Dad? We’re gonna need a bigger budget."

Mark Russell just leaned against the wall and sighed. "I'll go talk to the accountants. Tell them the Queen needs a Steinway."

Godric stood up, his heavy footsteps echoing as he walked toward Maria. He didn't say she was good. He didn't offer a compliment. He simply looked at the instrument in her hands, then back at her.

"Play it again," he rumbled, his voice like velvet over stone.

Maria's smile softened, turning private and warm just for him. "Only if you sit with me, Godric."

And the King of the Monsters, the Alpha Paramount, sat on the floor at her feet and listened as the Moon sang to the Sun in the language of a species they were both learning to call their own.
