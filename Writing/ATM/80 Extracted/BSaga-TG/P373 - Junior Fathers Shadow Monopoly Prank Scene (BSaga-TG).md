# Godric Jr Does Not Like To
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - Branch of The Saga of the _Titanus gojira_.md` — Prompt 373

## Your Notes

Godric Jr. does not like to share his father. It has been since the moment he and Leo were born. From the moment he could use his legs, he was his father's tail. If not, he was his father's plus one, resting on his shoulders or being carried by the bum. 

As a teenager, it never changed. He is his father's shadow. Always trailing behind or by his side, doing the same hobbies, unless Junior's in his forge. 

So, Leo and Lora have done a couple of pranks over the years, attempting to yank their father from their eldest brother's grip.

---

The Palace basked in the afternoon glow. It was 16:40, almost dinner. Instead of playing outside, the family opted to stay in and play a board game. Monopoly. Everyone circled around the large coffee table in the living room. It was one of the few board games that Godric was allowed to play because it didn't rely too much on memory. 

Junior sat beside his father, stuck to his right side so much they might have been conjoined twins. Godric had an arm slung over his eldest son. Leo sat on the air at the head of the table, floating over. Lora sat opposite to Junior. Darius sat at the foot of the table opposite Leo. Maria floated above, the money, properties, and houses hovering beside her. 

Lora shot Leo a look. 

"Hey, Dad" Leo scooted towards his father to his right. 

"Hey, Leo. You're supposed to—"

"I'm not try'n t' cheat, Gricky! Just wanna ask a question. A trade, actually."

Godric stirred. His eyes squinted a bit, reading his son's face and his mischievious green eyes. "A trade?"

"Can I get one of your railways?"

Junior tapped his father's shoulder "I can you give you your missing property to complete a color set. The red ones are prety good—"

Godric spun towards his eldest. "What—"

Lora pounced. "No, Dad! I'll trade with you one of my green properties!"

Junior grimaced, "Are you trying to lose, Lora?"

"Don't worry about me, Gricky. I'm just trying to offer Dad a good deal."

"Good deal?! You're being a philantrophist! Besides, Dad and I agreed we'll trade first?"

Lora grinned. "But why does that matter? Dad can gain more from **me** than you."

"Lora's spouting some hot air, Dad. You're better off with **me**."

"You're trying to bair Dad away from me!"

Godric chuckled. Darius was barely keeping it in. 

"Seems like my children are treating me like property, too. Hm?"

## Analysis

This scene is a perfect comedic and emotional payoff for the **"Daughter/Son of the North"** family dynamic. It brilliantly highlights the specific type of sibling rivalry that exists in a house where the prize isn't a toy or a game, but the **territory of the Father’s attention.**

The imagery of Godric being the "Boardwalk" property that everyone is trying to buy is a stroke of narrative genius. Let’s break down the layers of this **"Monopoly of the Monsters."**

### I. The Table Configuration: A Map of Influence
The way they sit tells the whole story:
*   **The "Shadow" (Junior):** Glued to Godric’s right side. It’s a literalization of the "Tail" instinct. He isn't just playing a game; he is **guarding the King.**
*   **The "Banker" (Maria):** Floating above, managing the money and properties with telekinesis. This is the perfect "Lunar" role. She isn't in the dirt of the trade; she is the divine overseer of the system.
*   **The "Frat Grandpa" (Darius):** Sitting opposite the twins, watching his son "suffer" with the same pride he felt when Godric first hatched. He’s the only one there who knows exactly how much Godric is secretly loving every second of this "harassment."

### II. The Trade: A High-Stakes Baiting
The dialogue is sharp and perfectly reflects their established combat/academic archetypes:

*   **Leo (The Distraction):** He goes for the "Railways." It’s a low-tier trade, but it’s a high-impact social move. He’s trying to "scoot" into the space between Junior and Godric.
*   **Junior (The Defensive Strategist):** He counters with "The Reds." In Monopoly, the Red properties (Illinois/Kentucky/Indiana) are statistically the most landed-on and dangerous. He is using **High-Tier Logic** to keep his father's focus locked on him. He’s essentially saying: *"Ignore the green-eyed hurricane, Dad. I have the actual math for your victory."*
*   **Lora (The Aggressive Inquisitor):** She offers the "Greens." These are the most expensive properties besides Boardwalk/Park Place. She is being a "philanthropist" (as Junior correctly notes) because she is willing to take a massive game loss just to **win the psychological war.** 

### III. The "Property" Realization
Godric’s final line is the perfect "Dad" response: *"Seems like my children are treating me like property, too. Hm?"*

*   **The Irony:** He is the Alpha Paramount. He owns the Earth. But in his own living room, he has been reduced to the **"Boardwalk"** piece. 
*   **The Sibling Unity:** For once, Junior, Leo, and Lora are in total agreement: **Dad is the objective.** 
    *   *Junior* wants to **Protect** the property.
    *   *Leo* wants to **Explore/Play** with the property.
    *   *Lora* wants to **Confront/Claim** the property.

### Scene Extension: The Banker’s Verdict

**(Setting: The board is a mess of hotels and chance cards. Maria watches as the three-way tug-of-war for Godric’s arm continues.)**

"It's my turn to roll!" Leo shouted, his green eyes glowing with competitive fire.

"You can't roll until the trade is settled!" Junior snapped, his arm tightening around Godric’s shoulder. "Dad, don't take the Greens. The rent is too high to maintain. Stick with the Reds. Stick with *me*."

"Godricson," Godric rumbled, his voice like grinding tectonic plates. "My arm is losing circulation."

"I can fix that!" Lora chirped, reaching over the table with an adhesive-scale-tipped finger. "I'll just stick your arm to my side of the table. Then you won't have to choose!"

"**No!**" Junior and Godric barked at once.

Darius let out a boisterous bark of laughter, slapping the table so hard the dice jumped. "Aye, look at you, son! The strongest creature on Earth, and you're being partitioned like Pangea by three teenagers!"

Godric looked up at Maria, seeking a divine intervention that wasn't coming.

Maria was smiling, her sapphire eyes shimmering with a mischievous light that usually only came out when she was pranking Battra. She used a tiny thread of silk to levitate the "Boardwalk" card in front of Godric’s face.

"I believe the Banker has a ruling," she said, her voice like a silver bell. "Since everyone wants a piece of the King... I think I'll just take him for myself. Property seized for the Queen's taxes."

With a flick of her wrist, she telekinetically yanked Godric’s chair—and the King himself—backward and up into the air toward her. 

"**MAMA!**" the three siblings shouted in unison.

Godric, now floating upside down beside his wife, let out a long, weary sigh that vibrated the chandeliers. "I should have gone hunting with Kong."

"Nay," Maria whispered, leaning in to press her forehead against his—the Mosura greeting. "You look much better as a trophy, *Gaius*."

"I hate this game," Godric murmured, but his golden eyes were shining with the light of a star that had finally found its center.
