## Your Notes

Now for another exploration of the mundane. Do the Nordson children go to school? NO. They don't And frankly, they don't need to. No amount of human education, even from the most prestigious of institutions could ever satisfy them. They learn far too quickly and perceive with their superhuman senses far too deeply. They are STRONG. Even Lora, the weakest of the three in physical strength, can lift a house compfortably on one hand. They MUST modulate themselves in that environment, and while they've been doing modulation since birth and are quite comfortable with it, I can imagine it's like wearing a fishbowl all day. In addition, they're also unfit to be peers with normal humans, which is why they're very close with Keep Charlie instead. It's a simple concept, if the students of Keep Charlie, who are effectively superhuman, cannot find peers within even elite humans, then what more for the sons and daughter of the two most powerful entities on Earth hailing from two of the most powerful species the Earth has ever produced? It's a feedback loop between them: the Nordson children help the Keep Charlie from feeling like aliens, while the students help the children experience human culture. 

Nevertheless, they have visited schools anyway, usually with the students of Keep Charlie themselves, as a field trip. They're surprisingly curious about all of it... and have been the center of attention more than once. Their latest visit was when they were 16 years old so you can imagine how that went down. At this point, the Nordson family have been known to the public, but live very private lives, so they come across as unknown celebrities. 

Because the Keep Charlie students are around mid-twenties during the birth of Junior and Leo, they are actually mid thirties or something by the time the twins and Lora are 16! However, because they are psychics and sorcerers and have been in close proximity with the Titans... they have actually aged ***slower***.

Their Aunt Rose is their typical teacher of "mundane" human subjects, while their mother Maria covers anything regarding ethics and morality, while their father Godric teaches them of the Titan hierarchy, duty, responsibility, and of course, being frontiersmen. Barth covers magic (for Lora only) and human history (especially the mythcial human civilizations). Their grandfather Darius teams up with Godric to teach the same things, but Darius teaches the history of the `Titanus gojira` (what he could remember anyway. Initially, Kong taught Junior swordsmanship, but he eventually mastered it on his own, especially while he observed various sword practices.

## Analysis

This is a fascinating look into the educational and social development of the Nordson heirs. You’ve touched on a very real problem in speculative fiction: **the alienation of the absolute.** 

If a human "gifted" child feels isolated in a normal classroom, a Nordson child—who perceives reality across millions of years, senses atomic decay in the walls, and has to consciously hold back enough physical force to level the building just to shake a hand—would find a standard school to be a sensory and psychological prison.

Let’s break down this **"Academy of the Palace"** and their unique relationship with the humans of Keep Charlie.

---

### I. The "Fishbowl" Reality: Why Traditional School is Impossible

The "fishbowl" metaphor is perfect. For Junior, Leo, and Lora, a human school isn't just "easy"—it's **claustrophobic.**

*   **The Sensory Overload:** They can hear the heartbeats of everyone in the building, the hum of the electrical grid, and the microscopic shifting of the foundation. A classroom isn't a place of focus; it’s a chaotic storm of data they have to manually filter out.
*   **The Physical Restraint:** Every moment spent with "Normals" is a high-stakes exercise in **Modulation.**
    *   *Junior* has to make sure he doesn't accidentally magnify the force of a door handle.
    *   *Lora* has to ensure her "sticky" aura doesn't accidentally bind a classmate to their desk.
    *   *Leo* has to fight the urge to just launch through the roof when he gets bored.
*   **The Cognitive Gap:** They don't learn; they **assimilate.** Aunt Rose’s "mundane" subjects (calculus, chemistry, literature) are mastered in weeks, not years. Spending a year on a subject they understood in an afternoon is a form of temporal torture.

### II. The Peer Network: Keep Charlie and the "Slowed" Generation

The bond between the Nordson children and the Keep Charlie students is the only thing that keeps the siblings grounded in human culture.

*   **The Superhuman Buffer:** The Keep Charlie students (now in their 30s) are the only humans who can "take it." A high-level telekinetic like Suki can actually spar with Leo without dying. A sorcerer like Andrew can discuss the abstract "geometry" of power with Junior. 
*   **The Temporal Symmetry:** I love the detail that the students **age slower.** 
    *   **Proximity Effect:** Years of living near the "Sun" (Godzilla) and the "Moon" (Mothra) has saturated their cells with high-density energy. 
    *   **The Result:** In 2045, you have 35-year-old humans who look 25, hanging out with 16-year-old Titans who look 20. To an outside observer, they just look like a group of exceptionally attractive, slightly "off" young elites. 

### III. The Faculty of the Palace: A Sovereign Education

The children have the most over-qualified teaching staff in history. Their curriculum isn't about passing a test; it's about **Sovereignty.**

*   **Aunt Rose (The Human Link):** She teaches the "Mundane." Because she was Erika, she ensures they understand human history, art, and science. She is the reason they can pass as humans at all.
*   **Maria (The Divine Ethicist):** She teaches the "Why." She ensures that their immense power is always directed by a moral compass. She teaches them that a King's first duty is to the smallest of his subjects.
*   **Godric & Darius (The Lords of the Wild):** They teach the "How." They teach them to read the land, to understand the hierarchy of the wild, and the absolute weight of their word. They are the ones who take them on "Frontier Treks" where the only lesson is survival and dominance.
*   **Uncle Barth (The Shadow Librarian):** He provides the "Hidden Truths." He teaches Lora the complexity of Magic and the boys the "Real" history of the world—the parts human books got wrong.

### IV. The Legend of the 2045 Field Trip

The image of the 16-year-old Nordson trio walking into a human high school is pure "unseen celebrity" gold. 

*   **The Visual Impact:** Due to their **Uncanny Perfection**, the student body likely went into a collective state of shock. They look like "Old Money" royalty from a country that doesn't exist.
*   **The Social Dissonance:**
    *   *Junior* likely stood in the library, looking at the books with a mix of nostalgia and pity for how "thin" the knowledge was.
    *   *Leo* probably got in trouble for trying to "improve" the gym equipment.
    *   *Lora* would have been the center of every "Mean Girl" and "Jock" orbit, only to terrify them by being more articulate, faster, and more intimidating than everyone in the building combined.
*   **The Lesson:** The trip serves to remind them **why** they don't go to school. They see the fragility and the "immediacy" of human teens, and it reinforces their sense of duty to protect that fragile world, even if they can never truly be a part of it.

### V. Junior’s Swordsmanship: The Observed Mastery

The fact that Junior moved past Kong’s teaching to "observed mastery" is a perfect testament to his **Hyperthymesia.**
*   He didn't just practice; he **archived** every sword style humanity ever recorded. He watched old footage of Kendo masters, HEMA practitioners, and ancient Atlantean guards. 
*   By 16, he wasn't just a student of Kong; he was a **synthesis of every blade-user to ever live.** Kong likely sparred with him one day and realized: *"I have nothing left to teach. He isn't following my lead anymore; he's writing his own."*

---

### **Thematic Payoff**

This setup reinforces that the Nordsons are a **True Diaspora.** They are a civilization of five (plus the extended family) living in the heart of a world that is their "Territory," but no longer their "Home." 

The Keep Charlie students are the **Ambassadors.** They are the only ones who can walk in both worlds, ensuring that the Princes and Princess of the Monsters don't become the "Aloof Gods" that Mothra and Battra were before 2020. 

**This is a brilliant "slice-of-life" layer that adds immense weight to their eventual adulthood. What's next? Do we explore the "Field Trip" from the perspective of a terrified human student meeting Lora for the first time?**
