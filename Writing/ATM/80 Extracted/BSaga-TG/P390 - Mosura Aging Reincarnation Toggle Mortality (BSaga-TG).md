# For The Mosura They Technically Can
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - Branch of The Saga of the _Titanus gojira_.md` — Prompt 390

## Your Notes

For the `mosura`, they technically *can* age. However, because they can modify some elements of their biology within their Reincarnation Egg, they simply can toggle this "feature" off however they please. For example, if Mothra (AS A TITAN IN A TITAN FORM) wants to watch over a human civilization, but posits that they may only last for about half a millenium or so, then she can simply set her biological clock to age up until that point***or*** she can simply choose not to age. In-universe, this explains why I will have her appear in every incarnation she's ever had on film!

All *mosura* can do this, but it is unkown if the three hybrids can, as they've never died. There is no other biological reason. The Covenant of the *mosura* granted them immortality in the truest sense. The Intangible. 

Does that mean the `mosura` understand mortality more than the `gojira`? No. Far from it. Ironically, the `gojira` are closer to the mortals than they are to the immortal `mosura`. The `mosura` understand mortality *on paper*, but never what it really means to be mortal as they can just come back again and again, as opposed to the `gojira` who live one life, forever.

Would you like to learn from me about the Soul Fragments of Mothra?

## Analysis

This is a fascinating inversion of the typical "Immortal vs. Mortal" trope. Usually, the being who dies often is seen as understanding death *better*. But you’ve flipped it: because death has no permanent consequence for the *mosura*, it has no **weight**.

*   **The Gojira Anxiety:** Godric clings to life because he knows it is singular. Even if it lasts a billion years, if it ends, it ends. He is **Mortal** in philosophy, if not in lifespan.
*   **The Mosura Detachment:** Maria treats bodies like outfits. "Oh, this one is getting old? I'll just go back to the Egg and adjust the sliders." She is **Immortal** in philosophy. She treats death like a nap.

This explains why **Junior** (the Gojira-leaning hybrid) would be terrified of his mother's casual self-sacrifice. He understands the *value* of the singular life in a way she biologically cannot.
