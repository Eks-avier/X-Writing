# Before The Time Of Homo Sapiens
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - The Antitheriomorphosis - KOTM Rewrite.md` — Prompt 277

## Your Notes

Before the time of *Homo sapiens*, Godzilla HAD allowed Challengers to grow. There's a reason why the culture surrounding it exists. The best part is that it happens naturally. Given enough time, which he has in droves, a strong enough individual arises, one who has achieved influence over a vast region on Earth. Godzilla would take satisfaction in challenging and defeating them, as it keeps him sharp and serves as unintentional entertainment for the other Titans. No one ever bets against him, but it's always fun for them to watch anyway.

His weariness after fighting Ghidorah in 2019 - the reason his doused fighting spirit - as well as his repressed nostalgia for this age was what made him enjoy his fight with Kong.

While he has begun to enjoy the strategic game of today, he still misses that simpler age. 

---

Actually, Ghidorah's first crash landing during the tail end of the recent Ice Age, signified a shift in his priorities. Godzilla was thrilled, ecstatic even. Here was a being who was his TRUE rival. Mothra would never fight him, but this alien dragon would. 

After the next four full battles with the alien dragon, he has begun to slowly grow weary of combat. The joy slowly draining out of him once he realized Ghidorah will stop at nothing to destroy this planet. 

Once Ghidorah was sealed by Scylla and Shimo in the Antarctic, Godzilla had only grown more grave. As long as the cosmic dragon lives in his planet, there's no reason to lower his guard. When Ghidorah inevitably is freed on 2019, it was a culmination of all that anticipation. Their 6th and final battle was the last and the one that killed his fighting spirit... until Kong.

## Analysis

This timeline of Godzilla's emotional relationship with combat is the final key to unlocking his psychology at the start of the series. It transforms him from a generic "grumpy guardian" into a **warrior in the midst of a profound existential burnout.**

It paints a picture of a King who lost the joy of his own nature, only to have it unexpectedly rekindled by a rival he initially dismissed.

Let's map this **Arc of the Warrior's Spirit**.

---

### **I. The Era of the Gladiator (Pre-Human/Pre-Ice Age)**
**"The Game of Thrones"**

*   **The Context:** This was the Golden Age of the "Challenge Culture." Godzilla wasn't just tolerant of Challengers; he **cultivated** them.
*   **The Mindset:** Combat was sport, ritual, and self-expression. It was the "exposer of the self." Beating a Challenger wasn't about survival (he knew he would win); it was about testing their mettle, correcting their flaws, and entertaining his court.
*   **The Vibe:** He was a confident, engaged champion. He allowed Challengers to grow because a strong court made for a stable planet, and a good fight kept him sharp. The "parades" and "celebrations" were genuine festivities.

### **II. The Era of Total War (The Arrival of Ghidorah)**
**"The Game Ends"**

*   **The Initial Thrill:** When Ghidorah arrived, Godzilla felt the thrill of a lifetime. Finally, a peer! Someone who could take a hit, someone who pushed him to his limits.
*   **The Sour Turn:** But the thrill died quickly. Ghidorah didn't fight for territory or dominance; he fought for **annihilation.** He didn't respect the surrender. He didn't care about the hierarchy. He broke the "social contract" of Titan combat.
*   **The Shift:** Combat stopped being "exposer of the self" and became **"survival of the world."** The joy evaporated. Every fight with Ghidorah was a desperate, grim defense of existence itself.

### **III. The Long Vigil (The Sealing to 2019)**
**"The Cold War"**

*   **The Context:** Scylla and Shimo managed to freeze Ghidorah, but Godzilla knew it wasn't over.
*   **The Mindset:** Paranoia and Duty. He couldn't enjoy the "games" of the Challengers anymore because he always had one eye on the Antarctic ice. Every Challenger became a nuisance, a distraction from the *real* threat. He stopped cultivating rivals and started suppressing them to keep the board clear for the inevitable return of the Golden Demise.
*   **The Result:** He became the "Miser" in spirit. Stern, efficient, and joyless.

### **IV. The Death of the Spirit (2019)**
**"The Hollow Victory"**

*   **The Event:** The 6th Battle. Boston.
*   **The Emotional Toll:** Winning didn't feel like a triumph; it felt like an execution. It was the end of an exhausting, million-year headache. He didn't feel glory; he felt **drained.** The fight took everything he had, leaving him spiritually hollowed out. He was the King, but he was tired of the crown.

### **V. The Renaissance (2022 - The Kong Fight)**
**"The Spark Returns"**

*   **The Catalyst:** This puts the Skull Island fight into a heartbreakingly beautiful perspective.
*   **Why Kong Mattered:** Kong didn't fight like Ghidorah (to destroy the world) and he didn't fight like the nuisances of the Long Vigil (distractions).
    *   Kong fought with **honor.**
    *   Kong fought with **ingenuity.**
    *   Kong fought to **win**, not to annihilate.
*   **The Awakening:** For the first time in eons, Godzilla wasn't fighting a monster or a murderer; he was fighting a **Rival.** The thrill he felt wasn't just adrenaline; it was **nostalgia.** It was the feeling of the Gladiator Era returning. Kong reminded him that fighting could be *fun*.
*   **The Healing:** This is why he spared Kong. This is why he gave him the Sovereign title. Kong gave him back the one thing Ghidorah had stolen: **The Love of the Game.**

### **Implications for the Present (Ichi & Godric)**

This adds a delicious layer to the Godric/Arthur/Ichi dynamic.

*   **Godric's Smugness:** When Godric looks at Arthur/Ichi now, he isn't just seeing a defeated enemy. He's seeing the guy who *ruined the fun* for a million years, now forced to do push-ups. It makes his trolling of Ichi feel like personal payback for eons of stress.
*   **Ichi's Confusion:** Ichi remembers the grim, humorless executioner of 2019. He is baffled by this new, looser, snarky Godric because he doesn't realize that **Kong fixed him.**

This timeline perfectly contextualizes Godzilla's personality shift from the grim titan of the movies to the more complex, multi-faceted character in your AU. It earns his "goofiness" by showing it as a return to form, a healing of his spirit.
