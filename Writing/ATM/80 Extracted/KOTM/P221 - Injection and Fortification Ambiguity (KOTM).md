## Analysis

This is an excellent line of questioning. You've pinpointed a crucial area that needs clarification to maintain the system's robust logic. The "resource allocation" system is brilliant, but we need to precisely define how an ultimate move like Injection interacts with it.

Your proposed solution is very strong, but we can refine it slightly to make it even cleaner and more thematically resonant. Let's tackle the two main questions.

---

### **Question 1: Where does Fortification fit into Injection?**

As we've defined it, Injection is a **hybrid** technique: it uses **Penetration** to deliver a payload of **Neutralization**.

So, where is Fortification? **It's not directly involved in the attack itself.**

However, Fortification plays a crucial **supporting role.** The physical limb that is delivering the Injection—the fist, the claw, the fingertip—must be able to withstand the incredible forces involved in piercing an enemy's defenses and depositing a volatile payload of concentrated will.

*   **The Answer:** A Kráton must maintain a sufficient level of **Fortification** on the limb delivering the Injection to prevent it from shattering on impact. If they sacrifice too much Fortification to boost their Penetration, they risk having their own arm explode just like Godric's early AA experiments.

This adds another layer to the "gambit" of Injection. It's not just a trade-off between offense and defense; it's a three-way balancing act between **Breaching (Penetration)**, **Payload (Neutralization)**, and **Structural Integrity (Fortification).** This makes the technique even more high-skill.

### **Question 2: Does 0% Neutralization mean you're performing Injection?**

No, and this is a key distinction. Allocating your Neutralization points away is a **separate tactical choice.** It's simply choosing to fight without active, will-based defenses, relying solely on your Fortification and base durability. This is the "Glass Cannon" stance we discussed.

**Injection is a separate, conscious, and *active* technique.** It is not the *absence* of defense, but the **weaponization** of defense.

Here's a clearer mechanical framework for Injection that integrates your ideas:

---

### **The Mechanics of Injection (`The Sovereign's Writ`): The Ultimate Expenditure**

Injection is the single most demanding technique in a Kráton's arsenal. It is not about reallocating points; it is about **spending** them.

1.  **Declare the Attack:** The Kráton consciously decides to perform an Injection.
2.  **Forge the Payload (The Sacrifice):** The Kráton takes a portion of their active **Neutralization** points and **forges them into a tangible payload.** Let's use your example:
    *   Godric is in a 20% AA state, with a **60-point budget** (20 Fortification, 20 Neutralization, 20 Penetration).
    *   He decides to create a moderately powerful Injection, sacrificing **10 points** of Neutralization.
3.  **The New Power Budget (The Vulnerability Gap):** These 10 points are now **locked** into the payload. They are gone from his active budget. His new, temporary power ceiling is now **50 points.** His active Neutralization is down to 10 points.
    *   This is the vulnerability gap. He is now operating at a reduced capacity because a chunk of his will is tied up in the "bullet" he is about to fire.
4.  **Deliver the Writ:** He then executes the attack, using his remaining Fortification and Penetration points. He might reallocate them as needed for this single strike (e.g., shifting points from Fortification to Penetration to ensure the hit lands).
5.  **The "Regeneration" (The Will's Recovery):** After the attack, the sacrificed points do not instantly return. They are an expenditure of will and energy. They begin to **regenerate** over time, at a rate dependent on the Kráton's core attributes (Willpower, Fitness, etc.).

#### **Why This is a Stronger Model:**

*   **It's a True "Ultimate":** It has a real, tangible cost. You don't just shift your stats around; you actively *spend* your power, leaving you weaker for a time. This makes the decision to use Injection a weighty, strategic one.
*   **It Prevents Spamming:** Because the points don't come back immediately, a Kráton can't just spam Injection. Using it once leaves them vulnerable. Using it multiple times could leave them completely drained and defenseless.
*   **It's More Thematic:** Reallocating is about strategy. *Spending* your power is about **sacrifice**. Injection feels more like a true "all-in" gambit if it has a recovery time associated with it.

This model is cleaner and gives Injection the high-cost, high-reward feel that an "ultimate ability" deserves. It perfectly incorporates your ideas of a vulnerability gap and a regeneration mechanic in a way that is logical and impactful.
