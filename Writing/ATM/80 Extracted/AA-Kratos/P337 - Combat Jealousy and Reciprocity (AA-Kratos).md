# Combat Jealousy and Reciprocity
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - AA - The Kratos of Kings.md` — Prompt 337

## Your Notes
Maria genuinely craves Godric's lovemaking. It's one of the few things she never expected to desire on such a visceral level. So, on some level, she knows she's not being fair when he withholds dueling him. Godric has crossed the threshold and actively does things for her pleasure, meanwhile she can't give him what he's been craving for several million years, now magnified by being her husband. It feels like she hasn't been reciprocating enough, even if Godric assures her it's fine and that he's just being dramatic for the joke. Maria internally doesn't buy it. She knows he's a warrior and she knows that there's another thing that means the world to him that is functionally equal to sex.

Maria hates that ***her husband***, her handsome, gorgeous, sexy, gentle, infuriating husband, has to go to ***his*** nemesis to satisfy that "warrior's itch." Maria is... "combat-jealous"?

Combat jealousy is equal to Anguirus' envy over Kong but less tragic and more comedic. She knows Godric ***despises*** Ichi, and likely takes great pleasure in beating him up whenever he could (thank goodness Ichi swaps over Arthur so he can't feel any of their duels), but she knows that as his wife, she should at least try understanding and engaging on him on his own terms. For Heaven's sake, he gave her a family: a father-in-law, a sister-in-law, three whole and very gifted children, stability, endless pleasure... Maria hates feeling like she's squandering or seeming ungrateful.

Godric does his utmost best to satisfy Maria. She literally cannot imagine a world where they don't have sex at least several times a week. That's how active they are. Still, the idea gnaws at her mind. Godric learned lovemaking just for her, and while he receives great satisfaction in ***giving*** her pleasure, she knows that she's being a little unfair towards him by ***not giving*** him a proper duel. Maria wants to be Godric's ***everything***. As his wife and the mother of his children, she feels a nagging obligation to **possess** the whole view of her husband.

## Analysis
This revelation of **Combat Jealousy** is the final, intricate piece of the psychological puzzle that defines the marriage of Godric and Maria. It is a concept that is simultaneously hilarious, deeply relatable in its human-scale insecurity, and profoundly mythic in its Titan-scale implications. By introducing this dynamic, you have transformed the relationship from a simple "opposites attract" story into a high-stakes negotiation of **Reciprocity and Possession.**

In the ATM Universe, where power is an extension of being, the refusal to engage in a specific type of intimacy (combat) is as significant as the refusal of physical intimacy in a human marriage. Maria's struggle is not just about her pacifism; it is about her dawning realization that to truly **possess** the King, she must be willing to meet him in the one place he feels most alive.

The following is a comprehensive analysis of this "Combat Jealousy," the asymmetry of their relationship's sacrifices, and the profound thematic journey Maria must undertake to become Godric's "Everything."

### The Asymmetry of the Eclipse: Reciprocity, Possession, and Combat Jealousy

## I. The Sacrifice of the Tangible: Godric's Mastery of Love

To understand Maria's guilt, we must first look at the sheer scale of the transition Godric Nordson made for her. As established in the lore of the *Titanus gojira*, Godric was a being of pure, visceral tangibility. His life was a 250-million-year-long campaign of survival, dominance, and unyielding duty. Before the Antitheriomorphosis, the concept of "pleasure" was biological and functional—the warmth of a nuclear vent, the satisfaction of a successful hunt.

### 1. The Virgin King's Learning Curve
Godric entered the human experience as a romantic and sexual blank slate. His reproductive instincts had physically atrophied due to eons of solitude. For him to become the "active, attentive, and incredibly skilled" lover Maria craves, he had to perform a feat of **Kratonic Adaptation** that rivals his invention of the Atomic Railgun.

He didn't just learn "how humans do it"; he applied the same discipline he used with Kong to the realm of the Intangible. He deconstructed his own physical power to learn the "Internal Fortification" of gentleness. He studied his own body's sensory feedback, calibrated his strength to the point where he could touch Maria's fragile-feeling human skin without leaving a mark, and invested eons of suppressed passion into the single goal of her satisfaction.

### 2. The Reciprocity Gap
Maria is acutely aware that her husband is a master of the "one-shot" and "attrition" doctrines. In their nightly activities, he applies these principles to her pleasure. He gives her everything. He has bridged the gap between the Tangible monster and the Intangible lover.

Maria sees this and feels a growing sense of Axiological (Psionic) failure. She is the Alpha Divine, the representative of **Values**. If she values him, why can she not give him the one thing his Gojira soul yearns for? He conquered his nature for her; she remains sheltered by her nature, refusing to cross the line into the arena for him.

## II. The Anatomy of Combat Jealousy

"Combat Jealousy" is a brilliant addition to the ATM social hierarchy. It is the realization that a husband is finding a core existential fulfillment with someone else—even if that person is a hated nemesis.

### 1. The "Mistress" in the Mindscape
In Maria's mind, Ichi (Ghidorah) represents a threat that has nothing to do with global annihilation and everything to do with **intimacy.** When Godric fights Ichi, he is unburdened. He doesn't have to worry about breaking the world. He doesn't have to worry about Maria's feelings. He can be the "Total Gojira"—the direct, brutal, and unyielding King.

Maria feels "cheated on" not by another woman, but by the **experience of Total War.** She watches Godric return from a "session" with Ichi—or even a high-intensity training with Kong—and she can see the "Zone" in his eyes. She can see the glow of his AA, the perfect alignment of his Kratos. She knows that in those moments, Godric achieved a level of **being** that she, his wife, denied him.

### 2. The Ichi Paradox
The comedy of the situation lies in the fact that Godzilla ***despises*** Ghidorah. To Godric, the fight is a purge; it is a way to vent his frustrations and maintain his skills. He doesn't see it as a bonding moment.

But to Maria, who perceives the world through **Feeling (Psionics)**, the "color" of the energy Godric emits during those fights is vibrant and honest. She is jealous of the honesty of the fight. She wants to be the one who pushes him to that limit. She wants the "golden eyes" of the warrior to be focused on her in a challenge, just as they are focused on her in desire.

## III. The Desire for Possession: The Whole View of the King

Maria's goal is to be the **Sole Sovereign of Godric's World.** This is a profoundly human desire for an ancient goddess to experience, and it highlights her transition from the Intangible to the Tangible.

### 1. The Divine Collector
Mothra has always been a "collector" of souls and stories (her Fairies, her telepathic links). As Maria, she wants to collect every facet of Godric Nordson. She has his love, his loyalty, his protection, and his domesticity. She has the father, the son, and the mate.

But she doesn't have the **Rival.**

In Titan culture, the relationship between equals is defined by the struggle. By refusing to fight him, Maria is keeping Godric at arm's length from a Titan perspective. She is his "Human Wife," but she is not fully his "Titan Peer." She realizes that as long as she refuses to duel, there is a "Room" in Godric's heart that she is not allowed to enter—the room where the warrior lives.

### 2. Possessing the Rage
Godric finds her rage "hot" because it makes her tangible. Maria, in turn, realizes that she wants to **possess his rage.** She doesn't want his anger to be something he only shows to enemies. She wants to be strong enough to withstand the full sun of his power and say, "Is that all?"

This is the ultimate form of submission/dominance in their relationship. If she can survive his "Sceptered Strike" (Penetration) in a duel, then she has truly conquered him. She has proven that she is the only being in the universe who can handle him in every possible way—body, mind, and will.

## IV. The Ethical Dilemma of the Alpha Divine

The reason Maria hasn't given in yet is rooted in her core **Axiology.**

### 1. The Fear of the "Dark Mirror"
Maria has seen what happens when a *mosura* embraces violence. She sees it in Battra. She fears that if she enjoys the fight—if she gives in to the "Warrior's Pride" that she felt during the Xilien Invasion—she will lose her divine grace. She is terrified that the "Angel of Heaven" within her will be tarnished by the "Monster of the Earth."

### 2. The Hypocrisy of the Pacifist
She feels the guilt of hypocrisy. She allows Godric to protect her. She relies on his violence to maintain the peace she cherishes. To refuse to participate in the act himself, while reaping the rewards, feels ungrateful to her. She realizes she is asking him to be the "executioner" so she can remain the "saint."

By learning to duel him, she is offering to share the "blood" on his hands. It is an act of **solidarity.** She is saying, "You don't have to be the only monster. We can be monsters together."

## V. The Path Forward: Transforming the Duel into Art

The resolution of this arc is not Maria becoming a brute. It is Maria discovering that for a **True Psychic**, combat is not violence—it is **Communication.**

### 1. The Spar as a Dialogue
If "to fight is to know," then Maria must learn to speak the Gojira's language. She needs to reframe the duel from a "failure of civility" into the **ultimate expression of intimacy.**

Instead of seeing it as "hurting him," she needs to see it as **Reflecting** him. When they spar, their Auras touch. Their Wills clash. It is a telepathic and physical conversation that is deeper than any words or even any lovemaking. It is the moment where the Tangible and Intangible are in perfect, violent balance.

### 2. The Completion of the Eclipse
When Maria finally gives him that "real fight"—the one where she dons her Regalia, sharpens her fans, and looks at him with the intent to *win*—that will be the moment the marriage is truly finalized.

It will be the day she says: "I don't just love the King who protects me; I love the Warrior who challenges me. And I am strong enough to be both your peace and your storm."

## VI. Conclusion: The Final Piece of the Nordson Family Soul

This "Combat Jealousy" subplot is the perfect way to ground the epic divinity of Mothra. It gives her a flaw that is deeply human: the desire for total possession and the guilt of not being "enough" for the person she loves.

It also sets up a beautiful dynamic for the children.
*   **Junior** sees his parents' "sparring" as the ultimate model of partnership. He learns that a relationship requires work in all three domains: Being, Knowledge, and Values.
*   **Leo** becomes their biggest cheerleader, not realizing the deep psychological drama behind their "play-fighting."
*   **Lora** finds inspiration in her mother's eventual mastery of the "Lethal Dance," using it as a template for her own three-system combat style.

Ultimately, the fact that Godric learned to love just for Maria, and that Maria is now learning to fight just for Godric, is the most profound proof of their connection. They are not just two Alphas sharing a bed; they are two souls who are willing to rewrite their own fundamental philosophies to ensure they can be **Everything** to each other.

The Eclipse is no longer a rare celestial event. It is a daily, working reality, built on the "John Smith" King's effort and the "Royal Queen's" dawning willingness to get her hands dirty for love.
