## Analysis
This refinement adds layers of **social injustice** and **triumphant perseverance** to the already powerful Fallen Star lore. It makes the simple act of touching a rock into the culmination of a lifetime of struggle.

### **I. The Outcasts at the Gate**

The added detail of *why* Dagon and Astraea couldn't touch the Star earlier is perfect tragic irony.

*   **Dagon (The Clanless Orphan):** The Star is a place of belonging, but Dagon had no lineage to claim. He was allowed to *witness* the ritual—to watch families pledge themselves and receive the blessing—but he was always on the outside looking in. His first touch of the Star was an act of defiance: "I have no clan, but I *made* one. I belong because I *willed* it."

*   **Astraea (The Caged Princess):** She was of noble blood, but her body was a prison. The Star was miles away from her sheltered cavern; the journey alone would have killed her. Every other Gojira made that pilgrimage in their youth. She had to wait until a stubborn orphan literally carried her there. Her first touch was a statement: "My body said no. My will said yes."

### **II. The Entwined Signal: A Love Stronger Than Entropy**

The idea that their wills are **bonded** in the Star is the emotional crescendo.

*   **The Mechanic:** Most individual wills dissolve into the collective "soup" over time. They become the background radiation. But Dagon (the Krator, whose will is uniquely strong) and Astraea (the Anomaly, whose will is uniquely *different*) created a stable pair. They locked together like atoms forming a molecule.
*   **The Result:** For 250 million years, their combined signal has been broadcasting a single, coherent emotion: the joy of their union. It is the single most powerful "broadcast" in the Star because it isn't just survival; it is *love*.

### **III. The Narrative Fork: Before or After Resurrection**

Your insight about the timing of Godric's discovery is crucial.

*   **Scenario A (Before Resurrection - Foreshadowing):**
    Godric touches the Star expecting to feel the grim, silent Dagon he knew. Instead, he feels a young man, terrified and thrilled, holding the hand of a woman who is radiating impossible hope. It *confuses* him. It plants a seed of doubt: "Who *was* my father, really?" This seed blooms into understanding when Dagon is resurrected and Godric finally sees the "Showa Soul" live.

*   **Scenario B (After Resurrection - Recontextualization):**
    Godric already knows his father is boisterous and loving. Touching the Star allows him to experience the *origin* of that personality. He feels the moment Dagon went from a defeated orphan to a triumphant husband. It gives him the "zero point" for the man he now calls "Dad."

### **IV. Darius's Return to the Star**

This is the tear-jerker you mentioned.

*   **The Setup:** After his resurrection, Darius eventually makes the journey back to the Star.
*   **The Touch:** He places his hand on the stone. He braces himself for the collective hum. Instead, he is immediately hit by a signal he hasn't felt in 250 million years: his wife's hand in his.
*   **The Moment:** His old imprint and Astraea's imprint are still bonded. When he touches the Star, he is, in a sense, completing the circuit. He feels *her*. Not a memory, not an echo—but the purest recording of her essence, calling out to him.
*   **The Dialogue (Internal):** *"You kept me waiting, my star,"* he might think. And for just a moment, in the silence of the Hollow Earth, it feels like she answers.

### **Conclusion**

This concept completes the "Tomb of Gold." The Star isn't just a source of power or a historical record; it is the **Mausoleum of the Marriage.** It is where Dagon and Astraea's love is preserved, waiting for their son—and eventually, for Dagon himself—to come home.
