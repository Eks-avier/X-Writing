### **Fleshed Out Scene: The King's Gift**

**(Setting: The Nordson's private lounge in the Palace. Lora's 18th birthday. The room is filled with a soft, warm light from Maria's bioluminescence. Maria, Battra, and the boys are presenting their gifts, but Godric stands back, a quiet, knowing smile on his face.)**

The day had been a whirlwind of joy and solemnity. Earlier, under the watchful, silent gaze of Heaven, Maria and Battra had guided Lora through the intricate and draining process of amending her Covenant. She had made her perfect sacrifice, trading the agelessness of her body for the boundless potential of magic. The air still thrummed with the residual power of the cosmic negotiation, and Lora, though visibly tired, was incandescent with a new, arcane energy.

Now, surrounded by family, she was receiving gifts. Junior had presented her with a stunningly crafted knife, a smaller, more elegant twin to the one their father had made for him, forged with his own hands. Leo, in a moment of rare sentimentality, had painted a breathtaking portrait of her, capturing not just her likeness, but the fiery spirit in her eyes. Maria's gift was the amendment itself, the most profound gift a mother could give: the freedom to choose her own destiny.

Finally, all eyes turned to Godric. He had yet to present anything.

"Well, Dad?" Lora asked, a teasing lilt in her voice, though her eyes were still heavy with the day's exertions. "Did you forget your own daughter's birthday?"

Godric simply smiled, that slow, quiet smile that crinkled the corners of his golden eyes. "Not at all, firecracker. I just had to prepare mine." He gestured towards the adjoining washroom. "Close your eyes."

A murmur of confusion went through the room. Lora shot a questioning glance at her mother, who just gave a slight, mysterious shrug, though her own sapphire eyes were dancing with anticipation. Lora closed her eyes, a grin playing on her lips.

They heard the soft click of a door, a few moments of silence, and then his voice, still that deep, velvety baritone, but somehow… younger. Sharper.

"Alright, lass," he said. "How's this? Does your Dad still got it?"

Lora opened her eyes. And gasped.

The man standing before her was a ghost from a photograph. The warm, comforting fortress of a beard was gone, revealing the sharp, perfect, and devastatingly handsome jawline she had only ever seen in old Monarch archives. His hair was still long, but without the frame of the beard, he looked impossibly regal, a king from a forgotten age. He was the man from the picture she had obsessed over as a child.

"DAAAAAD!" The word was a sob, a cry of pure, unadulterated joy that tore from her chest.

"Lora, I literally look just like that—" Junior started, his voice a mixture of amusement and exasperation.

"SHUT UP!" Lora shoved her brother aside without a second thought and launched herself across the room, flying directly into her father's arms. He caught her effortlessly, her smaller frame enveloped in his embrace. "Thank you, Daddy! Thank you!" she sobbed into his shoulder, clinging to him.

He held her tight, planting a long, gentle kiss on her forehead. "Happy birthday, my firecracker," he rumbled, his voice vibrating through her. "Your mother is going to absolutely strangle me for this, but I think it's worth it."

Lora pulled back, her face streaked with tears but beaming with a radiant happiness. She wiped her eyes and looked between her now clean-shaven father and her incandescently happy mother, who was watching them with a look of pure, unadulterated love. "We really decided to stress her to the max today, didn't we?" she giggled, before leaning in and planting a loud, affectionate kiss on her father's newly revealed cheek. "My decision… your beard."

"Aye, lass," Godric chuckled, his arms still securely around her. "At least we're together in facing the consequences." He gave Maria a playful, almost apologetic wink over Lora's head, a silent acknowledgment that he had broken their unspoken "no shaving" treaty for a very, very good cause.

He had given his daughter the one thing he knew she had always, secretly, wanted to see. And in doing so, he had given her more than just a gift; he had given her a tangible, visible sign of his pride, his support, and his unconditional love for the powerful, brilliant, and now, magical young woman she had become.
