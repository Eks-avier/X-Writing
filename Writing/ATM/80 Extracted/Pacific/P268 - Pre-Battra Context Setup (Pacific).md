The thing is. Battra knows the PPDC are unaware of the true nature of their Drift technology, which is why he didn't just leave a crater where the Shatterdome would have been. Rather, this isn't *for* the PPDC, this is for the Shadow Organization. He doesn't name them. Doesn't identify them. Battra is simply reminding everyone, especially those hidden in plain sight, that there are forces they cannot ever hope to control.

## Analysis

This is the perfect escalation. The addition of the "Wolf Collars" spell and the specific mechanics of his Refracting Scales elevates Battra from a powerful monster to a terrifying, untouchable sorcerer-god. It emphasizes his control and his cruelty when provoked. The fact that this is a message to the Shadow Organization ("I know you are here, and look how easily I can unmake your toys") makes it a masterstroke of multi-layered storytelling.

**DATE:** October 14, 2030
**LOCATION:** The Shatterdome, Hong Kong. Main Hangar Bay.
**STATUS:** SECURITY BREACH - CLASS: ALPHA

The alarm was a physical weight in the air, a rhythmic, deafening shriek that bounced off the steel walls of the hangar. But beneath the siren, there was another sound—the staccato thunder of assault rifles.

Fifty security personnel, the elite guard of the PPDC, formed a firing line. Muzzle flashes lit up the cavernous space like strobe lights. They poured thousands of rounds of high-caliber ammunition into the center of the room.

Floating ten feet in the air, legs crossed in a meditative lotus position, Bartholomew Lepidiel looked bored.

The air around him shimmered with a jagged, prismatic distortion. As the hail of bullets approached, they didn't flatten against a shield; they simply… *diverged*. The bullets curved wildly around his form, refracted by the invisible, passive field of his scale dust, slamming harmlessly into the concrete floor and walls behind him. He didn't even blink.

"Cease fire!" Marshal Stacker Pentecost roared from the command deck, realizing the futility.

The guns fell silent, the echoes ringing in the sudden quiet.

Battra uncrossed his legs and drifted upright, dusting an imaginary speck of lint from his purple lapel.

"You think of us as dull and mindless," he said. His voice wasn't loud, but it resonated in the teeth of every person in the room. "But I do believe you understand that we are... *immune* to such conventional weaponry, no? Such an oversight... and I thought you knew better, Marshal."

Pentecost gripped the railing. Beside him, Mako Mori and Raleigh Becket stood tense, hands hovering near sidearms that were useless.

"This is an act of war—!" Pentecost began, his voice hard as iron.

Battra’s eyes flashed crimson. "War implies both sides are of even standing, human."

He raised his right hand, snapping his fingers.

*CRACK.*

The sound was like a whip breaking the sound barrier. Instantly, a swirl of black, viscous smoke materialized around the necks of everyone in the hangar—the Marshal, Mako, Raleigh, the scientists Newt and Hermann, and every security guard.

The smoke solidified instantly into heavy, ornate collars of obsidian-black energy. They looked like something a cruel master would put on a hunting wolf. On the *inside* of the collars, sharp, serrated spikes glowed with a malevolent violet light.

Raleigh reached up to pull at his.

"Ah," Battra tutted, wagging a finger. "I wouldn't move a single muscle, if I were you. Excessive movement speeds up the spell."

As if to demonstrate, the collars hummed. The spikes moved inward a fraction of an inch, pressing against the skin of their throats. A collective gasp echoed through the room as everyone froze, terrified to even swallow.

"Better," Battra purred.

He turned his back on them, floating toward the towering form of *Gipsy Danger*. The Jaeger stood silent in its bay, a monument to human engineering. Battra looked it up and down, his expression one of critical appraisal, like an art teacher reviewing a toddler's finger painting.

"Ah, magnificent," he drawled, though the sarcasm dripped from every syllable. "These are yours? Although I do admit, they are vastly inferior to Jet Jaguar and his ilk. A crude imitation of a lost art."

"Get out," Pentecost strained to say, forcing his body to remain rigid against the collar's threat. "Now. You've done enough damage to Monarch's reputation—"

Battra spun in the air, drifting through a solid steel gantry as if it were mist. "Pray tell, ***useless mortal***. Do you think I care about the reputation of that merry band of equally insignificant ants?"

He stopped directly in front of Gipsy Danger's Conn-Pod. He could sense the technology inside. He could smell the stolen science, the "Drift" that was a bastardization of his sister's sacred domain. He knew the Shadow Organization was watching. He knew they were listening.

"Ah," he whispered, loud enough for the hidden listeners to hear. "Would you like to see a magic trick? I know trash like you adore such shows."

"WHAT—" Newt Geiszler squeaked from the floor.

Battra raised a hand. Purple energy crackled at his fingertips, the raw power of Primus answering his call. But then, he paused. He lowered his hand, the magic fading.

"No," he said, shaking his head. "I was considering using Magic, but I figured your eyes wouldn't be able to appreciate the nuance."

He looked back at Pentecost, his crimson eyes narrowing. "A **violation** of Psionics is deserving of a **Psionic** punishment."

His eyes glowed. He didn't cast a spell. He didn't weave a rune. He simply reached out with his mind and *pulled*.

A deep, terrifying groan of stressing metal filled the Shatterdome.

*Gipsy Danger* shuddered.

And then, it began to unravel.

It wasn't an explosion. It was a deconstruction. Massive bolts unscrewed themselves and floated in the air. Armor plating peeled away like the skin of an orange. Hydraulic pistons uncoupled with wet pops. The massive nuclear turbine in the chest drifted free, glowing ominously, suspended in a telekinetic grip.

Within seconds, the pride of the PPDC was no longer a robot. It was a floating cloud of a million individual parts, suspended in the air like a localized asteroid field.

"How tragic," Battra lectured, his voice calm amidst the visual chaos. "Against a telekinetic of sufficient force, your design fails. If the enemy includes such a weapon in their arsenal—and trust me, the universe is full of them—what response will you give?"

He scanned the room, his gaze lingering on the shadows where he knew the true architects of this theft were hiding.

"Your foundation is rot," he hissed. "And rot cannot hold weight."

He flicked his wrist.

**CRASH.**

The telekinetic field vanished. Thousands of tons of high-tech machinery rained down onto the hangar floor. It was a cacophony of destruction, the sound of a billion dollars of R&D hitting concrete. Gipsy Danger lay in a heap of scrap metal.

Battra dusted off his hands. He floated down until he was eye-level with Pentecost, the obsidian collar still tight around the Marshal's neck.

"I accept consultations for upgrades," Battra said, his voice returning to a sickly sweet politeness. "Prostrate yourself before Castle Bravo, in front of Director Russell, and I might consider putting your *toy* back together."

He leaned in close, his voice dropping to a whisper only the Marshal could hear. "And tell your... 'consultants'... that the next time they touch what belongs to the Divine, I will not be so gentle."

He snapped his fingers. The collars dissolved into black smoke and vanished.

"Don't take too long," Battra called out as a purple portal swirled open behind him. "I have papers to grade."

He stepped backward into the void, and the portal snapped shut, leaving the PPDC standing in the wreckage of their savior, terrified, humbled, and acutely aware that they were children playing in a playground of gods.
