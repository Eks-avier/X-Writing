### **Fleshed Out Scene: The Ghosts of Monarch Past**

**(Setting: The sprawling, sunlit living room of the Nordson's wing in the Palace. A quiet afternoon. Godric is on the floor, allowing a 12-year-old Leo to use his back as a drawing board. A 12-year-old Junior is meticulously organizing a collection of small, interesting stones on a low table. Maria watches them, a book resting forgotten in her lap, a soft smile on her face. An 11-year-old Lora is beside her, attempting to replicate one of her mother's intricate silk-weaving patterns.)**

Aunt Madison had visited earlier, bringing with her a data slate filled with what she called "ancient history"—Monarch archives from the early 2020s. Leo, in his usual hurricane-like fashion, had immediately started swiping through the files, his artist's eye looking for interesting images.

"Whoa!" Leo suddenly exclaimed, stopping his doodling on his father's back. "Hey! Look at this!"

He flipped the data slate around. Displayed on the screen was a formal photograph: the senior staff of Castle Bravo, circa 2021. And there, standing slightly apart from the humans, were two familiar, yet strangely different, figures. A man with startlingly blue eyes, short, severe black hair, and a clean-shaven jaw. Beside him, a woman with an ethereal beauty, her platinum hair pulled back into a tight, intricate braid that made her look like a warrior queen from a forgotten age.

"Mama and Daddy used to look like that?" Leo's voice was filled with genuine awe. "You're so good-looking!"

Junior abandoned his stones, leaning in to get a closer look. His analytical gaze swept over the image. "Mama looks so serious here..." he murmured, his brow furrowed.

"Daddy, too, right?" Leo added, pointing a finger at the screen. "And... why are your eyes blue?" He scrambled off his father's back to show him the picture.

Junior looked from the screen to his father's relaxed, golden-eyed face. "Don't ever swap back to your blue eyes in the house, Daddy!" he declared, the words coming out as a firm, childish command.

Lora, who had been focused on her weaving, looked up, her nose wrinkled in distaste at the image. "Hmph. I don't believe it." She turned to her mother. "No offense, Mama, but your hair looks so much better right now." Then, her voice dropped, becoming almost shy as she glanced at the man on the screen. "Daddy was SUPER handsome, though... Can I… um… see you without the beard?"

Godric, who had been silently observing the exchange with amusement, turned to his wife, a playful glint in his eye. "We'll have to ask the boss about that one, Lora."

Maria laughed, a bright, clear sound that filled the room. "It's non-negotiable, dear," she said, her hand coming to rest on Godric's bearded cheek.

"Oh well, firecracker," Godric said, a mock sigh in his voice as he looked back at his daughter. "You heard the boss."

Lora pouted, a full-on theatrical display. "Why NOT, Mama?! He looks so cool!"

"You looked dashing, Daddy!" Junior chimed in, his eyes still glued to the picture. He looked at his own clean-shaven face in a reflection on the table, then back at the image. A thoughtful look crossed his face. "Can this be my style from now on? I don't want you to ever remove your beard... I could just be the clean-shaven one. I could model for Lora instead!"

Godric paused, the playfulness in his expression softening into something deeper. He looked at his eldest son, seeing the earnest desire to honor him, to lift a burden. "If that's what you want, son," he said, his voice quiet but full of pride.

"EW! No way!" Lora immediately protested, her face scrunching up. "I want *only* Daddy to do it! He's far more handsome than you, Gricky!"

Leo, sensing the conversation shifting away from what he found interesting, piped up again. "But what if Mama ties her hair like that again? I bet she'd look SO cool!"

"Don't you dare do it, Mama!" Lora said, grabbing her mother's arm protectively. "You're so much prettier like this! Look at us, we match!" She gestured between her own loose, scarlet-tinged hair and her mother's river of moonlight. "Mama doesn't need braids to look cool, Leo!"

It was in that moment, in the swirl of his children's passionate, contradictory demands, that Godric felt a wave of pure, uncomplicated contentment. He looked at Maria, and a silent, amused conversation passed between them in a single glance. They were, he realized, no longer just themselves. They were a collection of memories and expectations, shaped by the loving, fiercely opinionated little beings they had created.

He summarized the new family decrees aloud, a hint of his Northern English accent surfacing in his amusement.

"So," he began, ticking off the points. "No shaving for me?"

Maria smiled, her sapphire eyes sparkling. "No braiding for me."

Junior nodded decisively. "No blue eyes in the house!"

Lora hugged her mother's arm tighter. "Yes, Mama! We'll always match!"

Leo, sensing the finality, delivered the last, most important rule. "And no flat faces!" he declared, poking his father's cheek. "Smiling only!"

Godric couldn't help but laugh, a deep, rumbling sound of pure joy. Trapped in a web of loving, ridiculous, and entirely non-negotiable demands, the King of Monsters had never felt so wonderfully, completely, and irrevocably conquered.
