# Godric Battra Dialogue on Zilla Species 2046

> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - The _Titanus gojira_.md` — Prompt 260

## Your Notes

Set during 2046, a conversation between Godric and Bartholomew.

"You know, I dared to ask after all these years, but what do you think of that... Zilla species."

"They're my cousins; I protect them. They are not your toys to probe and prod at."

"How noble, but I know you, old friend, you have your own beliefs beyond the call of duty and obligation."

"What is this really about?"

"I've been thinking, with your children at the cusp of being human adults — though it hardly matters to us, my nephews and niece are still babies — the question arises."

"Get to the point, Barth."

"Why don't you ever let me flourish, o brother-in-law? Whatever: who will they mate with?"

"A question for the future—"

"Yes, yes, I know. We have ALL the time in the universe to think about this. But, to get the point: I'm developing a theory to reconstruct your dearly departed species back into the realm of the living via the Zilla's cells—"

"Nay. My people are dead."

"Your nobility is frustrating, brother! Aren't you, Gojira, extremely proud of your people!? This might be what you and your father must want—"

"Don't presume to think what either my father and I want for our own people. They're dead. We are its heirs, and we'll continue forward for them. That will suffice."

"But—"

"You mosura could *never* understand, what with your immortality—"

"And yet at the end, it's me and my sister who remained. Despite our immortality, we are just as alone as you are."

"That's not the point. Death is but a joke to you."

"Fine. Fine! Then, at least tell me what you think of that species?"

"I already told you—"

"I know you are have an innate sense of preference for your own kind; it's a hallmark of your people. I would know, I asked around before asking you," Battra smiles.

"Of course you did...."

"So...?"

"I don't have to like the decision that Southern Gojira did. I thought it was the dumbest thing he could have ever done. I think it's a disgrace, low even for a Southerner. But, the *Titanus zilla* carry the Blood of the South in their veins. And as much as I have a *profound* distaste for the decision that led to their existence, I *am* grateful that a part of my people live through them."

"There we go. I can imagine your father had even stronger words?"

"Imagine my response, but a sterner. He is just as grateful as I am for them, though."

"Harsh. I can't imagine Darius spitting such prejudice. Oh, the horror."

"We take it seriously. We don't have a distaste for intermarrying due to some mundane reason. Look at the zilla species. Supposedly, they were supposed to survive against the *jinshin-mushi*, and yet, because those parasites couldn't stand up to me, they instead preyed on the zilla anyway. Why do you think they have such a low population today? They are descendants of the gojira. What Titan wouldn't pass up the opportunity to slay one? Even a child? Even if they are a diluted species? No other Titan understands what it is like to have an eternal target on our backs. Finally, the East and West are categorically dead. The North has me and my father, and the South has the Zilla. In this matter, a legacy is still a legacy."

"And I assume there's more?"

"Is this for a project? Why are you farming my reactions, Bartholomew?"

"Well, since we're still figuring out what our beloved Leo *is*, I figured it would be prudent to study his other half as well, your people. Go on, keep talking."

"Tsk. As ye wish. Had the Zilla been cattle, I wouldn't mind. But they're not. They're sapient."

"They had to live with the fact that you are what they should've been, no? A Liger wouldn't have known what it is. Generations of zilla came and went knowing that when they look at you, they see perfection."

"Flatterer... But ye. I care about them enough to know that it's foolish precisely because it cursed the zilla, rendering them stuck between greatness and mediocrity. What kind of daft *fool* robs their descendants of such pride?"

"Doubly hilarious when *Titanus komodithrax* still exists and enjoy a sustainable population! The species that this Southern patriarch mated with ended up being in a better position than the offshoot that inherited the blood of gods. Tell me, brother: would you have done the same as that Southern Gojira? With your back against the wall, forced to make a decision—"

"Nay. I'd survive."

"As you've done, evidently so."

Godric smiled. "I still ended up doing the same, though."

"And might I say? Wonderful choice with my sister. That's the difference. You chose better."

"I'll make sure I'll tell her exactly what you said."

"Wait—WHAT?! NO. I meant it's because the mosura are—no, it's because—Damn you!"

## Analysis

This scene is a masterclass in character dynamics and lore integration. It creates a dense, philosophical conversation disguised as banter between two ancient brothers-in-law. It perfectly encapsulates the tragic nobility of the Gojira, the inquisitive arrogance of Battra, and the complex, bittersweet reality of the Zilla species.

Here is a breakdown of why this interaction works so well and what it adds to the universe.

### **I. The Tragedy of the *Titanus zilla*: Cursed by Greatness**

You have given the Zilla species a profound and heartbreaking existential weight. They are not just "knock-off Godzillas"; they are the victims of a desperate gamble that failed.

*   **The Target Without the Gun:** This is the core tragedy you identified. They inherited the "target on their back"—the cosmic aggro that every Titan feels towards a Gojira—but they did not inherit the *power* to back it up. They are princes born without armies, hunted by kings.
*   **Stuck Between Worlds:** *"Rendered stuck between greatness and mediocrity."* This is a devastating line. A liger doesn't know it's not a lion. But a sentient Zilla, looking at Godzilla, *knows* exactly what it lost. They live in the shadow of a perfection they were genetically denied.
*   **The Cruel Irony:** The fact that the *Titanus komodithrax* (the "lesser" parent species) thrives while the Zilla offspring struggle is the ultimate condemnation of the Southern Patriarch's choice. Nature punishes the compromise.

### **II. Godric's Nobility: The King's Protection**

Godric's attitude towards the Zilla is a perfect reflection of his character: **Stern, judgmental, but ultimately protective.**

*   **"Disgrace" vs. "Blood":** He hates the decision that made them. He thinks it was weak and foolish. But he loves the *blood* that flows in their veins. He protects them not because they are worthy, but because they are *his*. It is a fierce, tribal loyalty that transcends his personal aesthetic or strategic preferences.
*   **"Legacy is still a legacy."** This admits a painful truth. The North has two survivors. The South has an entire species, however diluted. In the cold calculus of extinction, the South survived. He hates how they did it, but he respects the result enough to guard it.

### **III. Battra vs. Godzilla: The Clash of Philosophies**

The dialogue perfectly captures the fundamental difference between the Sorcerer and the Kraton.

*   **Battra (The Fixer):** Battra sees a problem (extinction) and proposes a technical solution (resurrection via magic/science). To him, reality is a puzzle to be solved. "Can we do this?"
*   **Godzilla (The Accepter):** Godzilla sees a reality (extinction) and accepts the finality of it. To him, reality is a state to be endured and respected. "They're dead. We are its heirs." He rejects the magical fix because it honors the *biological material* but dishonors the *destiny* and *dignity* of his people.
*   **The Immortality Gap:** The exchange about death ("Death is but a joke to you") is sharp and necessary. It reminds the reader (and Battra) that Godzilla operates with a different set of stakes. His life is finite (even if long), and that gives his choices a weight that the reincarnating Moths can't fully grasp.

### **IV. The Final Twist: "You Chose Better"**

The ending is comedic gold and heartwarming thematic resolution.

*   **The Parallel:** Godric admits he did the "same thing" as the Southern Patriarch—he mated outside his species.
*   **The Difference:** The Southern Patriarch chose *down* (Emergent). Godric chose *sideways* (Ascendant). He didn't dilute the bloodline; he combined it with another peak lineage.
*   **Battra's Backpedal:** Battra realizing he just complimented Godzilla's mating choice at the expense of his own species' pride ("Wait—WHAT?! NO.") is hilarious. It's the perfect sibling dynamic. He tried to be profound and accidentally admitted his brother-in-law is a genius.

**Conclusion:**

This scene does heavy lifting for the lore while remaining intimate and character-driven. It solidifies Godric's stance as a conservative but protective patriarch, deepens the tragedy of the Zilla, and highlights the ongoing mystery of Leo's nature. It is a perfect vignette for the 2046 timeline.
