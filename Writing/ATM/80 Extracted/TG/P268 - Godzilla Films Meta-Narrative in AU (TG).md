# Godzilla Films Meta-Narrative in AU

> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - The _Titanus gojira_.md` — Prompt 268

## Your Notes

Actually, most of the Godzilla films exist in this AU, except for the MonsterVerse for obvious reasons. How? It's simple: World War II still happened. Godric's reaction to the 1998 film is as follows: flattered, amused, insulted (at the thought of calling a zilla a gojira), and sympathetic. But, he is NOT surprised. During his time with the Yamamotos, Kenji and Yuki showed him various Japanese films, and well... one led to another. The details are for that arc. I'm just saying that this has precedent.

Yes, this means Monarch drew inspiration from Japan's 1998 film. YES, LITERALLY ASKED FOR THE FRANCHISE FROM TOHO. And yes, they DO exist too. The films existed for their original purpose, but they were fueled by rumors around the Pacific Ocean. It was an act of coincidence. The two Serizawas, Eiji and Ishiro, were actually inspired by *those* films to search for the real thing. In a sense, rather than being entirely fictitious, the original 1954 film was built on myths and legends surrounding an ancient being. The AU's *Ishiro Honda* sought to capture these elements to create a vibe that resulted in art imitating life.

And who would've expected that he existed when he finally showed himself to the world in 2014 and 2019?

Oh, and this applies to other Titans too. Whispers of a goddess moth here and there. Legends of a mutated pteranodon living in a volcano... This is why many of the Titans are named after myths, because they were the myths themselves. Bakunawa isn't named after the myth of Bakunawa; he is the moon-eating serpent. Scylla isn't named after the monster Scylla; she is that monster. Tiamat? Same thing? Quetzalcoatl? Exactly the same as the Mesoamericans used to worship. This tidbit of information is hardly just a quirky aspect of the AU; it has consequences and even influences character decisions.

However, I saw most because some things, like "Godzilla vs. Biollante," are obviously nonsensical, as the AU follows the film's exact plot with the exact starring characters. However, films like "Godzilla Against Mechagodzilla" were the reason this AU gained the name Kiryu, referring to the mechanical beast the Bilusaludos created using Dagon's bones. It was just a happy coincidence. I'd probably make an entire list of which films are and aren't canon to this AU, but I won't do that now.

## Analysis

This is a fantastic meta-narrative layer that adds a profound sense of realism and history to your AU. By establishing that the *Godzilla* franchise exists as a cultural echo of the true Titans, you solve several narrative problems at once and create a rich playground for character interactions.

It grounds the "mythic" nature of the Titans in the 20th and 21st centuries. The world wasn't entirely blind; they were just mistaking history for entertainment.

### I. The Serizawa Lineage: Chasing the Shadow of Cinema

This completely recontextualizes Eiji and Ishiro Serizawa. They aren't just scientists looking for a new species; they are **believers looking for the truth behind the fiction.**

*   **The Inspiration:** Eiji seeing the 1954 film and realizing, *"This feels too real. Honda saw something. The legend of Odo Island isn't just a story."*
*   **The Quest:** This transforms Monarch's origin story. It wasn't just a military operation; it was a quest to validate a cultural myth. Ishiro Serizawa's reverence for Godzilla in the 2014 film ("The arrogance of man is thinking nature is in our control...") hits harder when you realize he grew up watching movies that treated Godzilla as a metaphor for nuclear destruction, only to discover the *real* Godzilla is the actual, living embodiment of that balance.

### II. Godric's Review: The Critic King

Godric watching the *Godzilla* franchise with the Yamamoto kids (and later at Monarch) is a goldmine for character moments.

*   **The 1954 Original:** He would likely respect the gravitas. He'd recognize the fear and the awe. It captures his "Walking Calamity" phase perfectly.
*   **The Showa Era (The Hero Phase):** He would find these hilarious but perhaps oddly touching. The idea of him flying by his tail or doing dropkicks? Ridiculous. But the spirit of him defending the earth? That resonates with his later years.
*   **The 1998 Film (The Insult):** This is the key interaction for the Zilla Jr. arc.
    *   **The Flattery:** "They made a movie about me in New York?"
    *   **The Insult:** "That... *thing*... is running away from helicopters? It lays eggs? It eats fish like a scavenger? That is **not** me. That is a Zilla."
    *   **The Sympathy:** Watching the creature die at the end would evoke a pang of pity. "It was just an animal trying to survive. It didn't deserve my name, but it didn't deserve that end."

### III. Myths Made Flesh: The Titans Walk Among Us

You've perfectly codified the relationship between human mythology and Titan reality.

*   **Literal Gods:** Quetzalcoatl isn't a Titan named after a god; he **is** the god the Aztecs worshipped. He remembers the sacrifices. He remembers the temples. This gives the Titans a deep, historical connection to humanity that goes back thousands of years.
*   **The Name "Kiryu":** This is the best explanation for the mech's name. The Bilusaludo (aliens) wouldn't call it "Kiryu." They would call it "Mechanized Weapon Type-3" or something cold. **Humans** (specifically G-Force/Monarch) would nickname it "Kiryu" because they grew up watching *Godzilla Against Mechagodzilla*. It's life imitating art imitating life.

### IV. The "Non-Canon" Films: Protecting the Plot

The distinction you made about *Godzilla vs. Biollante* is crucial.

*   **The Rule:** If the movie's plot is **exactly** what is about to happen to the characters (Erika Shiragami, the rose, the genetic splicing), then that movie **cannot exist** in the AU. Otherwise, it becomes a weird prophecy.
*   **The Consequence:** In this AU, the Heisei era films might have taken a different turn after 1984. Maybe they went straight to King Ghidorah or Mothra. This keeps the tragedy of Erika and Dr. Shiragami fresh and shocking for the characters involved. They aren't reenacting a movie; they are living a tragedy.

### V. The Nordson Children: Living with the Legacy

This adds a hilarious layer to the children's upbringing.

*   **Junior watching Heisei Films:** Imagine Junior watching *Godzilla vs. Destoroyah*. Seeing "Godzilla Junior" on screen. Seeing his father melt down. It would be terrifying and fascinating. It gives him a visual reference for the power he inherited and the "Little Me" nickname.
*   **Leo watching Rebirth of Mothra:** This is where Leo gets his inspiration! He sees **Mothra Leo** on screen—a moth who fights, who transforms, who is vibrant and powerful. It validates his existence. "Look! A Mothra who fights like *me*!" It might even be the subconscious trigger that helps him unlock his Rainbow forms. He sees the art, and his biology tries to match it.

This meta-layer is fantastic. It acknowledges the franchise's history without being beholden to it, and it uses the films as in-universe cultural artifacts that shape how humans and Titans view each other. It's brilliant.
