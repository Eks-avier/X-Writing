### **Fleshed Out Scene: The Unwitting Infection**

**(Setting: The same cavernous, pristine training facility. The air is still and heavy with unspoken accusation.)**

The words hung in the air between them, a declaration as solid and heavy as the impossible weights on the racks. *"You've done something to me."*

Kong's every instinct screamed *threat*. He braced himself, a lifetime of combat against Skullcrawlers and worse telling him what came next. But his recent, bruising education in human culture offered a different, more confusing interpretation. He took a breath, the air tasting of sterile metal.

"You know," he began, his voice a low rumble, testing the waters. "Before you decide to rip my throat out, I just want to say... you're a lot scarier in here than you were back on the island."

He expected... something. A flash of anger. A move to close the distance. Bosses, he reasoned, punished insubordination. He had been his own boss since his parents died; the protocol was unfamiliar territory.

Instead, Godric chuckled.

It wasn't a sound of humor. It was a low, guttural vibration that thrummed through the floor, a sound of profound, weary irony. For a few seconds, it echoed in the huge, empty space. *This gym is incredible,* a small part of Kong's mind noted, a brief flash of a warrior's appreciation.

"You've... changed me," Godric said, the words coming out slow, as if he were describing a foreign pathogen.

A flicker of understanding. "Is that... a good thing?" Kong asked, genuinely unsure.

"I don't know."

The man was being obscenely obtuse. A king of a planet, and he spoke in riddles. *Has he always been like this?* And then, with a jolt of recognition, Kong saw it. The frustration. The inability to articulate a feeling that was too big for words. It reminded him of himself, struggling to explain a lifetime of loneliness to Jia with just his hands. And in that moment of shared, inarticulate frustration, he understood.

"You want my help?"

Godric didn't answer with words. He *moved*.

It wasn't an attack. It was a demonstration. A slow, fluid sequence—a low stance, a weaving parry, a feigned jab. And with a cold dread, Kong recognized every single motion. *By my forefathers, those are my moves. The ones from the island.*

It clicked. Then it clicked again, with even greater force. The old Iwi stories, the ones he'd only half-listened to, resurfaced in his mind. Tales of the Surface God, ancient and terrible. One legend said he had eaten a blue star. Another, more obscure one, whispered that he was a master of time. *A master of time? No... that couldn't be literal.*

But what if "time" was just their word for... *learning*? What if he could adapt, could learn in an instant? *The longer the fight, the better he becomes.*

*That's it.*

A third, simpler, more terrifying thought pushed its way through. *Or maybe he's just really, really old, and has seen it all before.* Kong let out a slow breath. He really wished he had paid more attention to the Iwi elders.

He looked at Godric, who now stood still, his body a perfect, poised replica of a stance Kong himself had used. He saw the conflict in the man's eyes, the deep-seated confusion of a master craftsman forced to use another's tools. And the fear and intimidation Kong had felt moments before evaporated, replaced by a sudden, exhilarating spark.

He was the only one who could have caused this. And he was the only one who could help fix it.

"Fine," Kong said, a slow grin spreading across his face. "I'll help you out." He took a step forward, no longer a subordinate, but a consultant. A peer. "We can figure this out. Together."

For his entire life, he had been the sole guardian, the lone warrior. He had never had a true sparring partner. As he looked at the confused, powerful, and utterly unique being before him, a giddy sense of possibility replaced everything else.

This wasn't a summons. It was an invitation.
