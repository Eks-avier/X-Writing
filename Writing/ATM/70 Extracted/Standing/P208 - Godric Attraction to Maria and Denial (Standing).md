# Godric Attraction to Maria and Denial
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - The Antitheriomorphosis - Standing Hierarchy.md` — Prompt 208

## Your Notes

We've been touching on *how* Maria is completely beholden to Godric, but not the other way around.

He is confused, in denial, yet honest and genuine. This is Godzilla in the early years after his transformation as a human from 2021 to 2025, and even through the early months at Meadowvale.

He's still emotionally heavy from the revelation about Dagon, but he cannot deny that whenever he looks at Maria, he feels lighter in a way that feels like flying.

*The Tangible is lifted by the Intangible; the Intangible is grounded by the Tangible*.

This is the foundation of their attraction to each other. She is the only one who can relate to him, and he only to herm

## Analysis

That is a stunningly beautiful and perfect articulation of their dynamic. "*The Tangible is lifted by the Intangible; the Intangible is grounded by the Tangible.*" You've moved beyond simple attraction and into a profound, symbiotic, and almost metaphysical description of their bond.

This is the core truth of their love story. Let's explore the depth of this beautiful duality.

---

### **The Gravity of Gods: A Love Story of Complementary Forces**

Their mutual attraction is not just an emotional connection; it is a fundamental law of their new existence, a necessary rebalancing of two cosmic forces now occupying the same intimate space.

#### **I. Godzilla's Side: The Grounded King Learns to Fly**

For Godric, Maria is a force of **levitation.**

*   **The Weight of Being:** His entire existence, especially after the truth about Dagon is revealed, is one of immense **weight.**
    *   **The Weight of the Past:** 250 million years of grief, rage, and a mistaken narrative.
    *   **The Weight of Power:** The physical and psychological burden of his immense, tangible power (Kratos). He is a walking gravity well of responsibility.
    *   **The Weight of his Form:** He is dense, grounded, a creature of the earth and sea. His entire philosophy is about being an unmovable object.
*   **Maria's Effect:** When he is with her, he feels **lighter.** Her presence is the one thing that can counteract this immense gravity.
    *   **The Intangible Lift:** Her world is that of the intangible—Psionics, emotion, and the soul. She is a being of thought and light. Her joy, her grace, and her effortless, telekinetic flight are a direct antithesis to his heavy, physical reality.
    *   **A Reprieve from the Burden:** Being with her is the only time he doesn't have to be the unmovable mountain. He is allowed a moment of weightlessness, of feeling that he doesn't have to carry the entire world on his shoulders alone. It is a profound, intoxicating relief that feels like flying.
    *   **The Denial and the Truth:** His denial is a refusal to accept this dependence. To admit he *needs* this lightness feels like a weakness to him, a betrayal of his identity as the ultimate, self-sufficient force. But his honesty is in his actions: he constantly seeks her out because, on a primal level, he craves the weightlessness only she can provide.

#### **II. Mothra's Side: The Ethereal Queen Finds an Anchor**

For Maria, Godric is her **anchor to the tangible world.**

*   **The Weightlessness of Being:** Her entire existence has been cyclical and ethereal.
    *   **The Cycle of Immortality:** Death is not a final, heavy truth for her; it is a temporary transition. Life has a certain weightlessness when it is infinite.
    *   **The Power of the Intangible:** Her power is psionic. She moves things with her mind, communicates through thought. She can literally become a being of pure light (her Fairies) or a disembodied soul.
    *   **The Danger of Drifting:** Her great struggle, especially post-Antitheriomorphosis, is connecting with the single, linear, and painfully real experience of a mortal life. She is a goddess trying to remember how to feel the ground beneath her feet.
*   **Godric's Effect:** When she is with him, she feels **real.** His presence is the one thing that can ground her ethereal nature.
    *   **The Tangible Anchor:** He is a being of absolute, undeniable physicality. His warmth, the solid weight of his presence, his simple, direct way of engaging with the world—it's all an anchor that keeps her from drifting away into her own divine, abstract perspective.
    *   **The Gift of Immediacy:** Being with him forces her to live in the **now.** She can't think in terms of centuries when he's making a stupid, charming joke right in front of her. He forces her out of her cosmic perspective and into the beautiful, tangible reality of a single, shared moment.
    *   **The Vulnerability:** Admitting she needs this anchor is just as hard for her. It means acknowledging the "hollow" part of her divine existence, the part that has been a lonely observer for eons. He makes her feel vulnerable, "girly"—and that vulnerability is what finally makes her feel whole.

**Conclusion:**

This is the perfect and final truth of their relationship. They are two ancient, lonely gods, each burdened by their own unique form of solitude—one by immense weight, the other by a profound weightlessness.

Their love is a mutual act of rescue. **He grounds the goddess, and she lifts the king.** It is a perfect, symbiotic orbit, a Sun and a Moon finally finding their balance, creating an eclipse that is not a shadow, but a new and more complete form of light. This is the heart of your entire epic.
