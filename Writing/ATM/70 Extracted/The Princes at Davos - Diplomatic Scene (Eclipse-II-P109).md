### Developed Scene: The Princes at Davos

**Part 1: The Doomed Delegation**

"Dad," Junior's voice was tight, the precursor to a migraine he could feel gathering behind his temples. "Is there absolutely no chance you can just come with me?"

His father, the King of Monsters, a being who had leveled civilizations, gave him a dismissive shrug from the terrace doorway. "I'm busy. Your grandfather, your uncle, and I have… business. In the Hollow Earth."

Junior's composure, so carefully maintained, finally cracked. "AND THAT'S MORE IMPORTANT THAN THE WORLD ECONOMIC FORUM?!"

Godric let out a low chuckle that rumbled through the room, before it bloomed into a full, genuine laugh. "You'll be fine. Stand next to the Monarch delegation, look intimidating, and what is that ridiculous phrase you youngsters use? *Aurafarming*? Yes. Do that."

*I am going to strangle Leo.* The thought was so clear and precise, Junior half-wondered if his brother felt a psychic chill halfway across the Palace. *How does Dad know what that is?!*

"Please," he said, the word coming out closer to a beg than a command. *It's going to be so unbearably awkward.* "If what you, Papa, and Uncle Kong are doing is just going *hunting*, then can't I pass on this one, too?"

"What makes you think we're going hunting, young man?" his father asked, raising a single, questioning eyebrow.

"You can't fool me," Junior shot back, a spark of defiance in his voice. "I saw you talking to Uncle Kong on that phone you claim you never use."

"Well," Godric said, completely ignoring the accusation, "if you don't want to go alone, I suppose we can get Leo to accompany you."

The blood drained from Junior's face. "NO, DADDY! THAT'S EXPONENTIALLY WORSE!"

A third voice, cool and amused, drifted from the sofa. "Would you rather I go, instead?" Lora was sipping a vibrant green juice, a picture of detached interest. *For someone so eager for a fight, this is when she decides to speak?!*

"NO," the two Nordson men replied in perfect, immediate unison. "Absolutely not."

Lora laughed, a bright, clear sound. "Well, guess it's Leo for you, Gricky."

"Dad—" he began, turning to plead with his father one last time.

"Bah," Godric said, already stepping out onto the terrace. "You're a prince of the world. Figure it out." The glass door slid shut, leaving Junior alone with the crushing weight of impending diplomatic doom.

**Part 2: The Art of the (Social) Disaster**

Two hours later, standing amidst the polite, high-powered hum of the pre-summit reception in Davos, Junior's worst fears had been realized.

"*Leo?!*"

His younger brother was a radiant beacon of effortless charm, holding court with a young woman whose rapt expression was a mixture of awe and hopeless infatuation. Junior felt the headache begin its final, punishing assault.

He opened their private telepathic link, his mental "voice" a tight, controlled hiss. *"Leo, what in the name of the Fallen Star do you think you're doing?!"*

Leo didn't even flinch, his smile widening as he gestured vaguely in Junior's direction. "Ah, well, that's my brother," Junior heard him say aloud, his voice warm and inviting. "He's really handsome, right?"

*"That does it!"*

Junior strode through the crowd, a sleek, white-haired shark parting a sea of dark suits. He was vaguely aware that people were moving out of his way with an almost reflexive deference, their subconscious minds registering a predator long before their conscious ones saw a handsome young man.

"Apologies," he said, his voice a low, polite baritone as he reached the pair. "I need to borrow my little brother." He didn't wait for a response, his hand closing around Leo's arm with a bit more force than was strictly necessary.

"Yo! Where're we—"

"*Not. Here.*"

He towed Leo into a secluded alcove behind a massive marble pillar, the sound of the reception becoming a distant murmur. He finally let go. "Did you even know who that was you were talking to?"

"Uh, no?" Leo replied, rubbing his arm with a frown. "But she was kind!"

Junior felt the overwhelming urge to place his palm directly through his own forehead. "Weren't you listening during the debrief with Aunt Madison earlier?"

*"Of course I was! I'm not dumb, Gricky!"* Leo's mental voice was a flare of indignation. *"I was just making conversation. Just because you're content to be a brooding statue in a corner doesn't mean I have to be!"*

"*I get that, Lee,*" Junior sighed, the fight draining out of him as he leaned against the cold marble. "*But you can't be cruel to them.*"

"*Cruel?! What are you talking about?*"

Junior finally gave in, pressing his palm to his forehead with a groan. "*Were you born with telepathy, or is there a switch in that beautiful, empty head of yours that is currently in the off position?*"

*"I don't understand—*"

"*Of COURSE you don't! You're an open, walking flirtation! You are leading that poor woman on! Look at the way she's still staring at you, you dork! To her, you're not just being friendly; you are a prince showing interest!*"

Leo fell silent for a long moment, the boisterous energy draining from his psychic presence. Junior watched as his brother's expression shifted from confusion to a dawning, horrified understanding.

*"Oh,"* Leo's mental voice was small, filled with a guilt that pained Junior to sense. *"Oh, no. Was I... was I doing that all evening?*"

Junior's anger evaporated, replaced by a wave of familiar, protective exasperation. He dropped his hand and straightened up. "*Mostly, yes. Lee, you radiate sincerity. When you show someone that much warmth and attention, they think it's special. For you, it's just your default setting. For a human, it's a once-in-a-lifetime experience. It's not fair to them.*"

Leo looked genuinely distressed. *"So what am I supposed to do? Be a jerk?"*

Junior offered a small, rare smile. "No. You just need to be more aware." He clapped his brother on the shoulder. "And I," he sighed, "probably need to stop hiding behind pillars. Dad's right. I need to figure it out." He looked back out at the glittering crowd, a sea of calculating, ambitious minds. "Come on. Let's go 'aurafarm' together. We'll be statues, but at least we'll be statues together."

Leo brightened instantly. *"Deal. But if someone interesting comes along, I'm making you do the talking."*

"*It's a start,*" Junior thought, a sense of calm finally settling over him. Perhaps this diplomatic mission wasn't doomed, after all. It was just another kind of hunt, and for the first time that night, he felt ready to face it—with his chaotic, impossible, but utterly indispensable brother at his side.
