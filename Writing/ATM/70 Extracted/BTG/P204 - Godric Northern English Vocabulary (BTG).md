## Analysis

### The King's Vernacular: The Language of the North

When Godric slips into his true, comfortable accent, his vocabulary shifts. It becomes more textured, more direct, and imbued with a quiet, ancient poetry. Maria is right; it is ridiculously charming.

#### I. Terms of Endearment & Familiarity

These are the words that surface most often, reserved for his family and closest friends.

*   **Aye / Nay:** As established, these are his defaults for "yes" and "no." They are short, definitive, and ancient.
*   **Lad / Lass / Lassie:** His go-to terms for younger people he is fond of. He'd call Junior "lad" in a moment of paternal pride. He might refer to Madison as "the lass" with grudging affection. It's a term of gentle, almost paternalistic endearment.
*   **Wee:** A classic Scottish term for "small" or "little." He would use it with a touch of irony or genuine affection.
    *   *"Careful with that **wee** teacup, lad."* (Said to Junior, with a knowing smile).
    *   *"It was just a **wee** scuffle."* (His likely description of a city-leveling battle).
*   **Bairn:** A Northern English and Scottish word for "child." He would likely use this only in his most private, unguarded moments when referring to his own children, especially when they were very young. The word is heavy with a deep, ancient tenderness.
    *   *"The **bairns** are sleeping."*
*   **Ken:** The Scottish word for "to know." He would use this in a rhetorical, almost philosophical way.
    *   *"You **ken** how it is."* (A statement of shared, unspoken understanding).

#### II. Descriptors & Adjectives: The Color of His World

His descriptions become more earthy, more elemental.

*   **Dour:** A Scottish word for "gloomy," "sullen," or "stern." He would use this to describe a grim situation or even his own past self with a dry sense of humor.
    *   *"It was a **dour** winter that year."*
    *   *"Your grandfather and I were in a rather **dour** mood."*
*   **Braw:** A Scottish term for "fine," "good," or "pleasant." Used to express simple, genuine appreciation.
    *   *"That's a **braw** piece of work, lad."* (Said to Junior in the smithy).
*   **Baltic / Nesh:** Northern English slang for "very cold" or "sensitive to the cold," respectively. He would use these with a deep sense of irony, as he is immune to the cold.
    *   *"Are ye feelin' a bit **nesh**, Roman? Put a jumper on."* (Said to a shivering Rodan).
*   **Clarty:** A Geordie/Northern English word for "very muddy" or "sticky." A perfect, earthy word he'd use after a trek in the Hollow Earth.
    *   *"The ground's all **clarty** down there today."*

#### III. Verbs & Actions: A More Direct Speech

His action words become more direct and visceral.

*   **Fash:** Scottish for "to worry" or "to trouble oneself." He would use it to dismiss concerns.
    *   *"Dinnae **fash** yersel', Maria. He'll be fine."* (Don't worry yourself, Maria).
*   **Haud:** Scottish for "to hold."
    *   *"**Haud** on a moment."*
*   **Gie:** Scottish for "to give." Used in a very direct, almost commanding way.
    *   *"**Gie** it here."* (Give it here).
*   **Mind:** Used in place of "remember" or "be careful of."
    *   *"**Mind** your step."*
    *   *"I **mind** the day my father left."* (I remember...).

#### IV. The Complete Voice in a Scene

**(Original)**
> "I still can't believe you're such a terrible goof."
> "Hah, you haven't seen anything yet. Your mother adored my mischief."

**(With the Northern Vernacular)**
> "I still can't believe you're such a terrible goof."
> "Hah," Darius chuckled, a low rumble. "You haven't seen anything yet, lad. Your mother adored my mischief. A **braw** lass, she was."

**(Another Example: The Quarrel with Maria)**
**(American English)**
> "Are you really telling me how my own scales work?"
> "It's cool how you can basically fix bones like that."
> "I hate you."

**(Northern English/Scottish)**
> "Are you really telling me how my own scales work?"
> "Aye. It's braw how you can fix bones like that."
> (Maria rolls her eyes, a hint of a smile on her face) "I hate you."

The effect is subtle but profound. It makes his voice feel older, more grounded, and infinitely more intimate. It is the sound of Godric Nordson at home, a quiet, rumbling current beneath the crashing waves of the King of the Monsters. It's the voice Maria fell in love with, a sound that speaks of ancient mountains, cold seas, and an unshakable, enduring strength.
