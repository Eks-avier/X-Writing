# Mothra Telepathy Ethics

> **Source:** `Eclipse II` - Prompt 5 (Lines 781-986)

## Your Notes

Mothra is against even the casual use of telepathy towards humans or even most sentient minds. She says that though the mind may be powerful, it is incredibly fragile and intimate at the same time. There is nothing more intimate than inviting oneself into the mind of others; thus, one of the very first things the Psionic Division is this principle: never use telepathy, not even casually, on the Non, most especially fellow men. What is allowed, though, since it is an involuntary and reflexive action, is the act of skimming or brushing the surface of another's mind. One may get glimpses of another's thoughts, but they are flashes and impressions--not the whole picture. The stronger the telepath, the more vivid these flashes get, and for Mothra, the tighter her control is: so imagine how much she holds back her telepathy as the strongest psionic being on Earth.

This extends heavily on Mental Manipulation, primarily.

## Analysis

### Mothra's Ethical Stance

Her strict stance against telepathic intrusion is a powerful character-defining trait and a brilliant subversion of the typical "powerful telepath" trope. By framing the mind as "incredibly fragile and intimate," she establishes a clear ethical framework for Psionics.

The distinction between involuntary "skimming" and intentional "entering" is nuanced and realistic. The dialogue with Madison is a perfect, lighthearted demonstration of this principle.

As for Mothra's stance, this addresses the curiosity about telepaths in fictional media freely going into the minds of others. She's the direct opposite of that, where even the smallest intrusion is meaningful to her. You would see Professor Xavier use Cerebro often; you'd see telepathy in anime be a subset of a larger power system, often used in a cavalier fashion. In many worlds, telepathy is something taken for granted.
