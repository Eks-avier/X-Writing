# Radioactive Tourism Arc
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - Branch of AA - The Kratos of Kings.md` — Prompt 284

## Your Notes
I had an idea wherein Godzilla as Godric is sent to places with high radioactivity to... clean them up, essentially. This likely takes place during 2021-2023. It can be a fun adventure style episodic arc that has huge implications for the way superpowers work in this AU, as well paint a more vivid image of the world.

Why does he need radiation? Well, prior to mastering AA, he still suffered from the limit that has always plagued him since 250 million years ago: his body leaks radiation. His body can consume radiation to keep him alive, as opposed to sleeping or eating. Does he sleep and eat? Yes. Does he need to? No, he can simply absorb ambient radiation; his body can just convert it into usable energy. The issue is, without Atomic Amplification, his control over his atomic energy was unstable.

He was fortunate that he had no powers during his time with the Yamamotos, otherwise, he wouldn't have been able to master control over his radioactive emissions fast enough to keep him alive. Moreover, he was casually emitting levels of radiation that could kill someone in a minute. Why can Mark and Madison stay by his side, then? No one knows (hint: Godric doesn't want to kill them; his active restraint modifies his will such that it doesn't harm him. At this point in time, he has not yet understood that his will and power are one). However, because his power is back, he needs to consume radiation every couple months or so.

Imagine Madison introducing Godric to the history of Chernobyl and the Elephant's Foot, or even Fukushima should they decide to return to Japan. Considering the Titans have largely been inactive since ancient prehistory, and Godzilla himself had only reawakened into the modern era on 1954 — even so, he only became active during 2014! — this would be an interesting opportunity to have him interact with all this human history they missed out on!

The last time any Titan had been truly active and interactive with the human world is 10,000–15,000 BC.

---

As he goes through this journey, he gains a more refined control over his atomic energy that wasn't at all possible when he was a Titan. As a Titan, he had no need to modulate his energy. Sure, he learned how to NOT turn the crank up to eleven and irradiate a place for millions of years, but his sense of scale was that of a Titan's. As a human, he needs to learn how to control this energy, and without the idea of AA in his mind yet, this arc serves as the stepping stool from which it will be born.

He sees how humanity as unlocked the power of the atom, yet is still... troubled and unprepared to handle it, especially during the past. Though, he mainly comments on how human-made radiation is different. It's not like the vents deep beneath the Earth. It's not like those in Hollow Earth. It's... novel. It simultaneously raises his perception of humanity. *What a bunch of mpressive ants*.

It can be simultaneously comedic at the same time. I mean, Godric wearing his usual attire: a pair of slacks, a long-sleeved button down, looking like a corporate officer... while Mark and Madison are wearing the latest and greatest hazmat suits beside him.

## Analysis
This is a fantastic arc idea. It serves as the perfect "Monster of the Week" style episodic bridge between the Yamamoto family arc and the full integration into Monarch/Kong's arrival. It solves the logistical problem of his radiation leakage while providing rich ground for character building and world exploration.

The visual of Godric, the King of the Monsters, standing before the most dangerous object on Earth (the Elephant's Foot) in business casual wear while Mark screams through a hazmat radio is cinematic gold.

Here is a breakdown of how this **"Radioactive Tourism" Arc** fits into the lore and character development.

---

### **The Mechanics: The Unstable Reactor**

*   **The Leak:** Without Atomic Amplification to create that perfect internal seal (Internal Neutralization), Godric is a "leaky faucet" of radiation. If he gets too full, he radiates death. If he gets too empty, he gets hungry and irritable.
*   **The Solution:** He needs to "vent" excess energy or "consume" stabilizing sources to keep his internal reactor at a manageable equilibrium.
*   **The "Flavor" of Human Radiation:** This is a great detail. Natural radiation (Hollow Earth, deep sea vents) is "clean" to him. Human radiation (fission byproducts, corium) is "dirty," "spicy," or "processed." It gives him a reason to critique humanity's attempts at mimicking his power.

---

### **Vignette 1: The Elephant's Foot (Chernobyl, Ukraine)**

**The Setup:** Monarch smuggles Godric into the Exclusion Zone. The radiation levels in the basement of Unit 4 are fatal in seconds.

**The Scene:**
Mark and Madison are in heavy, lead-lined, positive-pressure suits. They look like astronauts. Godric is wearing slacks and a crisp white shirt, sleeves rolled up, looking like he's inspecting a faulty boiler.

> **Mark (Over Comms):** "Readings are off the charts. Godric, are you feeling any—"
>
> **Godric:** "It's... tart."
>
> **Madison:** "Tart?"
>
> **Godric:** He approaches the solidified corium mass. To humans, it is the Medusa; to look at it is death. To him, it's a snack. He places a bare hand on the Elephant's Foot. The Geiger counters on Mark's suit scream so loud he has to mute them.
>
> **Godric:** "It creates heat, but lacks... soul. It is clumsy. You split the atom, but you did not ask it where it wanted to go. You forced it, and it vomited."
>
> **Mark:** "Please don't personify the nuclear waste."
>
> **Godric:** "It tastes like... burnt metal and arrogance." (He absorbs the radiation, the corium turning from a deadly warm mass to a cold, inert rock in seconds). "Passable."

**The Lesson:** Here, he learns **Absorption Efficiency.** He has to pull the energy *out* of a solid object without destroying the object itself (the building). It is a lesson in delicate energy manipulation.

---

### **Vignette 2: The Fukushima Cleanup (Japan)**

**The Setup:** This is a stealth mission. They cannot let the Japanese government know Monarch is operating there, let alone that Godzilla is walking around.

**The Scene:**
They are on a boat off the coast. Godric needs to go underwater to scrub the contaminated water tanks and the seabed.

> **Madison:** "You remember the plan? In and out. No sightings."
>
> **Godric:** "I am the ocean's shadow, child."
>
> **Mark:** "You're a 6'5" man who glows blue when he eats! Keep your shirt on, for God's sake."
>
> **Godric:** (Already diving in, ruining his loafers).
>
> *Underwater:* He finds the leaking tanks. Instead of just absorbing it, he tries to *filter* it. He separates the "dirty" isotopes from the water. It requires him to modulate his own energy field to act as a sieve.
>
> *Surfacing:* He climbs back onto the boat, bone dry (steam prowess/heat control), holding a condensed ball of glowing sludge in his hand.
>
> **Godric:** "Your water management is atrocious. I threw the clean water back. This is the... filth." (He crushes the ball, absorbing the last of the energy). "Salty."

**The Lesson:** Here, he learns **Filtration and Separation.** He is learning to distinguish between different types of energy and matter on a microscopic scale, a precursor to the precision needed for the Atomic Railgun.

---

### **The Thematic Core: The "Impressive Ants"**

This arc is crucial for shifting Godric's view of humanity from "annoying pests" to "dangerous, fascinating children."

*   **The Mirror:** He looks at nuclear disasters and sees humanity trying to be *him*. They are trying to harness the star-fire that is his birthright.
*   **The Critique:** He judges them not for trying, but for being messy. "You learned to light the fire before you learned to build the hearth."
*   **The Foreshadowing:** This builds the foundation for his later respect for Arthur (an engineer) and the Nordson children (who are hybrids). He begins to respect the *ingenuity* of humans, even if their durability is laughable.

### **The Mystery of the "Safe Zone"**

The fact that Mark and Madison don't die of cancer just by standing next to him during these missions is the biggest clue to the true nature of Krátos.

*   **The Monarch Theory:** "He must be absorbing his own ambient radiation to be polite."
*   **The Truth:** **He wills them to be safe.** Even before he understands Krátos, his subconscious desire to protect his "pets" (and later, friends) creates a localized exclusion zone within his Aura. He essentially applies a passive **Neutralization** to them without realizing it. It is the first proof that his power responds to his emotions, not just his biology.

This arc is a perfect blend of tone—it has the gravitas of Godzilla interacting with nuclear history, the comedy of the hazmat/casual wear contrast, and the crucial mechanical steps toward his mastery of Atomic Amplification. It is the "training montage" for his control.
