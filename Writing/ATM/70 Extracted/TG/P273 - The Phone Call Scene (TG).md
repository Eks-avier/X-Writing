### The Call of the King

**Location: The Warehouse, Queens**
**Time: 14:00**

The silence in the warehouse was heavy, broken only by the hum of N.I.G.E.L.'s cooling fans and the frantic tapping of Mendel's keyboard. The massive screen on the wall still displayed the two waveforms: the jagged green scrawl of Zeke's energy, and the terrifyingly perfect, oscillating blue sine wave of the intruder.

Then, a sound cut through the tension like a knife.

*Riiiiiing.*

It wasn't a cell phone. It was the dusty, rotary red phone sitting on Nick's desk—the secure analog line that hadn't rung since Ishiro Serizawa went dark in 2014.

The team froze. Randy stopped chewing his gum. Monique's hand drifted to the sidearm concealed in her jacket.

"That's impossible," Elsie whispered. "That line is dead. The routing codes were scrambled years ago."

*Riiiiiing.*

Nick stared at it, paralyzed by the implications. If that phone was ringing, it meant someone with Serizawa's deepest secrets was on the other end.

Zeke didn't hesitate. He moved with a fluid, predatory grace that belied his human appearance, snatching the receiver from the cradle before Nick could stop him. He didn't put it to his ear; he hit the speaker button, his eyes narrowed, nostrils flaring as if he could smell the caller through the wire.

"Yeah?" Zeke barked, his New York accent thick with defensive bravado.

For a moment, there was only the crackle of static. Then, a voice filled the room.

"Hullo?"

It wasn't a question; it was a rumble. Deep, textured, and resonant, it sounded like heavy stones grinding against each other deep underground. It carried a thick, rolling accent from the north of Britain, utterly incongruous with the situation yet commanding absolute attention.

Then, a sharp *click*. The line went dead. The dial tone hummed, mocking them.

Zeke slammed the receiver down, his chest heaving. "He's here."

"Who?" Randy squeaked. "The Scotsman?"

"The Blue," Zeke snarled. He grabbed his jacket, his eyes flashing with a dangerous, bioluminescent green. "He's not just in the city. He's *close*."

He bolted for the roll-up door, moving faster than a human should.

"Zeke, wait!" Nick shouted, grabbing his gear. "Team, move out! Don't let him engage alone!"

**Location: A Black Pickup Truck, Three Blocks Away**

Godric stared at the sleek smartphone in his hand as if it were a venomous insect he had just crushed. He resisted the urge—barely—to fling the device out the window and into the East River.

Instead, a low, gravelly chuckle escaped his chest, rumbling against the steering wheel.

*Ridiculous.*

Nobody had ever told the Alpha Paramount that phone calls could be so distinctively embarrassing. There was a reason he usually refused to touch the expensive, military-grade device Madison had forced upon him ("It's for emergencies, Gric, not for coasters!").

He looked at the number he had dialed. It wasn't saved in his contacts; he didn't use contacts. He had pulled the sequence of digits from the pristine, photographic archive of his mind—Page 1,042 of Ishiro Serizawa's journal, a scribbled footnote in the margin titled *'Emergency Contact - T. Site Omega.'*

His conscious mind, the part of him that was learning to navigate human logic, had told him it was a fool's errand. *They would have moved. The line would be disconnected. Serizawa has been dead for years.*

He had called it anyway.

"Yeah?"

The voice on the other end had been young, brash, and edged with a defensive growl. It wasn't a scientist. It wasn't a soldier. It was the voice of a street fighter expecting a challenge.

Godric tossed the phone onto the passenger seat. His brain told him it was a wrong number, a coincidence.

But his gut? His gut told him he had hit the jackpot.

If Godric followed his brain half as much as he followed his instincts, he wouldn't be King of the Monsters. He wouldn't have survived the Permian, the Ice Ages, or Ghidorah.

He shifted the truck into gear, the engine growling in sympathy with its driver. The energy signature he had been tracking—that faint, diluted, green scent of Southern Gojira blood mixed with something else—was flaring. It was getting stronger, hotter, and it was moving rapidly towards him.

"Right then, lad," Godric murmured, his blue eyes shifting, just for a second, to a flash of molten gold. "Let's see what ye're made of."

He turned the wheel, driving straight toward the oncoming storm.
