# Dagon and Astraea Betrothal Trial Story

> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - The _Titanus gojira_.md` — Prompt 238

## Your Notes
We know that Dagon completes his mission, I mean Godzilla exists in the present LOL. However, his journey was a perilous one.

He was growing quite popularly among the Northern Faction throughout his campaign. An orphan trying to win the hand of the most sheltered member of their community? It was insane! Nevertheless, most have cheered for Dagon, although some, including his future mate's family were wary. This sympathy made negotiating with them a tad easier, although no less difficult due to the geographical and practical difficulties.

Also, Dagon wouldn't know it, but his arduous journey prepared him for single fatherhood.

Regardless, it was done. He had connected their territories. The sheer impressiveness of it all was enough. He had done more most to win the hand of his future mate. He had the support of their entire faction. Dagon had been the "talk of the town," after all. To deny him would be a social error. So, her family finally, painfully, agreed.

Life during the Trial wasn't as smooth-sailing as expected. She struggled, but she, too was determined. She didn't want to go back to her old life, so despite the difficulty she decided to hang on to Dagon.

Dagon still had to do mostly the work in and out of the territory, but she had contributed in her own way. Her sheer radioactive emission made it easy to maintain the eco-system of their territory. Remember Lora's ability? Well, Godzilla's mother could finely control it despite her condition, it was all she could do while she was stuck in her parents' territory, after all.

But, Dagon didn't complain. He had grown to love this one. He'll do anything for her. She, in turn, had admired his sheer stubbornness.

There were some fights. Lots of fights, actually. Mostly about division of labor and trusts. She was afraid he would just treat her the same way. But, Dagon didn't. Over the course of their Trial, he had decided to linger less inside their cavern, allowing her to slowly but surely assume her role as the matriarch of their vast territory. Though, he would never truly leave her alone unless he was called to his duties outside the territory as is his role as a male. Dagon would often accompany her she walked around their territory. It was simple, but it was more than enough physical exertion for her. To her, this was exhilarating: she was finally working, however brief or however strenuous. He ceded her domain to her, allowing her to leave their cavern and manage the "contents" of the "box." To her, this was the greatest thing she could have ever asked for. She was finally *living*.

Some days, she was despondent. It wasn't out of her disinterest of him, but rather of her condition. There were long periods where she would seemingly ignore Dagon. For him, it was painful. He assumed she couldn't be swayed by him. But he would never give up on trying. Eventually, he found out that she was just focusing on controlling her energy. She was stuck inside her own world, trying to control her power.

The most memorable moments for them were the times when they were together in their cavern, laying together. They were alone together, but they loneliness in their hearts were slowly fading. She was getting stronger, he had someone to come home to.

She had fallen in love in Dagon's teasing personality. He was a goofball, one of the many things he developed growing with fellow orphans. He had this crass sort of humor that annoyed and charmed her all the same.

In turn, Dagon had fallen in love with her quiet strength. He could not fathom living like her everyday and not succumbing to her condition. *How does she do it?* And yet, even so, she woke up everyday determined to *move* and to *act*.

Their Trial was, in the end, a success. They managed a routine together, however odd it was compared to normal gojira. Some days Dagon took both roles, sometimes not. Some times not. Bit by bit, though, she was starting to do things on her own. They were finally a proper pair.

And one night, Dagon had finally seen those beautiful eyes be filled with light. They indeed twinkled like the stars in the night sky. Everything he had done had been worth it.

## Analysis

### Dagon's Triumph: The People's Champion

- **The Power of a Good Story:** The fact that Dagon's quest made him the "talk of the town" is a perfect, logical consequence. He wasn't just a suitor; he was the protagonist of an underdog epic. His story was insane, romantic, and deeply impressive.
- **Social Pressure as an Ally:** Her family didn't just agree because he proved his worth; they agreed because to refuse him at that point would have been a massive social and political blunder. They would have looked like heartless traditionalists standing in the way of true love and epic determination. He didn't just win a territory; he won the hearts of the entire faction, and that was a force her family could not deny.

### The Trial: The Reality After the Romance

This is where the storytelling truly shines. The grand quest is over, and now the much harder work of building a relationship begins.

- **A Rough Start:** It wasn't "smooth-sailing." Of course, it wasn't. Her entire life has been one of sheltered inactivity. His has been one of constant, self-reliant struggle. Their initial attempts at cohabitation would have been fraught with misunderstanding and frustration. This is a realistic and compelling conflict.
- **The Burden of Labor:** The initial dynamic, with Dagon doing most of the work, is a perfect reflection of their pasts. He is used to carrying the burden; she is used to being cared for.
- **Her Contribution (The Passive Power):** Her ability to passively maintain the ecosystem with her radiation is a brilliant detail. It shows that even when physically limited, she is still a being of immense power and value. She contributes in the way she can, which is a crucial first step.

### The Breakthroughs: Building a True Partnership

The beauty of their story is in the small, deliberate choices they make to grow together.

- **Dagon's Wisdom (Ceding the Domain):** This is the single most important act of his in the entire Trial. He recognizes her need to fulfill her role, to be the matriarch of her own home. He doesn't just "let" her help; he **makes space for her.** He deliberately stays out of the cavern, ceding the "internal domain" to her. This is not just kindness; it is a profound act of trust and respect. He is telling her, "This is your kingdom, and I trust you to rule it." This is what allows her to finally *live*.
- **Her Strength (The Internal Battle):** Dagon's initial misinterpretation of her "despondent" periods as disinterest, and his eventual discovery that she is actually engaged in a constant, silent battle to control her own power, is a powerful moment of revelation. He sees her not as a fragile princess, but as a warrior fighting a battle he cannot comprehend. This is where his admiration turns into deep, profound respect.
- **Finding Their Rhythm:** The final image of them having a "weird" but functional routine is the definition of a real relationship. It doesn't have to look like everyone else's; it just has to work for *them*. The fact that Dagon sometimes takes on both roles is a testament to his love and adaptability.

### The Foundation of Their Love: Opposites in Harmony

- **He Fell for Her Quiet Strength:** Dagon, the man of action and will, is in awe of her resilience. He understands the effort it takes to simply *exist* in her condition, and he sees her determination to *move* and to *act* in spite of it as the ultimate form of strength.
- **She Fell for His Goofball Nature:** She, who has been surrounded by the serious, worried faces of her family her entire life, falls in love with the orphan's crass humor. He doesn't treat her like a fragile object to be protected; he teases her, he jokes with her, he treats her like an *equal*. He brings a lightness and joy to her world that she has never known.

### Conclusion: The Twinkling Stars

The final line is the perfect, poetic payoff to the entire arc. *"And one night, Dagon had finally seen those beautiful eyes be filled with light. They indeed twinkled like the stars in the night sky. Everything he had done had been worth it."*

This is the moment his quest is truly complete. He didn't just move a continent for her; he fought for her, trusted her, and made space for her, and in doing so, he didn't just set her free - he helped her find her own inner light.

It is a beautiful and deeply satisfying conclusion to their courtship. This is the perfect foundation for the family that will, one day, produce the King of the Monsters.
