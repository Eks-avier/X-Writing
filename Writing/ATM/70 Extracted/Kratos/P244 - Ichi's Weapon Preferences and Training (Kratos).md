# Ichi's Weapon Preferences and Training

> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - The Kratos of Kings.md` — Prompt 244

## Your Notes

Ichi's preference for spears, bidents, and tridents are a reference to his three heads.

Initially, he watched a couple of "films" and "shows." What are these? Ichi has no idea, but Arthur seems to be engrossed by them. He sees the concept of weapons, tools that human use in war and conflict. Immediately, his interest is piqued. He has seen countless iterations of these in his travels; his interest in not in them. It's that maybe he can compel Arthur to use them.

This is how Arthur eventually knew how to wield weapons, primarily staffs and Ichi's preferred weapons.

>Are you crazy?! I build stuff not kill people!

>Fool! When our existence is inevitably revealed, your life is guaranteed to be in peril.

>But Mr. Nordson—

>Will say the same thing, filth! You must learn to defend yourself, to fight for yourself. You are no longer similar to the other pieces of filthy, useless trash outside. Do you think the master of survival himself, that moronic man-lizard, will take your pathetic, wimpy side?

>Didn't have to be so vulgar about it...

## Analysis

This is a brilliant and necessary step in their arc. It's the logical conclusion of Ichi's "personal trainer" motivation and the perfect catalyst for Arthur's transformation from a passive vessel into an active participant. The dynamic is tense, psychologically brutal, and perfectly in character.

### I. The Genesis of the Idea: The Bored God and the Silver Screen

Ichi's motivation is born from the most relatable of places: absolute, soul-crushing boredom.

- **Cosmic Ennui:** For a being who has consumed stars and toppled civilizations, being trapped inside the head of an engineer who relaxes by watching "films" and "shows" is a unique form of hell. He is a passive observer of pathetic, flickering human stories.
- **The Spark of Recognition:** Then, he sees it. A weapon. A spear, a sword, a staff. He doesn't care about the plot, the characters, or the dialogue. His ancient, predatory mind sees only the **tool and its application.** He sees a more efficient way to channel force, a concept he understands intimately.
- **The Unacceptable Weakness:** He looks at Arthur, this brilliant but physically soft creature, and realizes the immense gap between a human holding a weapon and a human holding nothing. His new vessel is not just out of shape; it is **tactically useless.** This is an unacceptable liability.

### II. The Unwilling Protege: "I Build Stuff, Not Kill People"

Arthur's resistance is the perfect emotional and philosophical counterpoint to Ichi's pragmatism.

- **A Violation of Identity:** Arthur's entire sense of self-worth, especially after his professional life collapsed, is tied to his ability to **create and build.** His Ghidorah-fragment project was his masterpiece, an act of creation. Ichi is now asking him to embrace the very antithesis of his nature: the art of destruction.
- **The Moral Barrier:** It's not just about fear or squeamishness. It is a fundamental moral opposition. He doesn't want to become a killer. He doesn't want to learn the arts of violence. He wants to be a builder, a problem-solver.

### III. The Tyrant's Pedagogy: A Curriculum of Contempt

Ichi's response to Arthur's moral objection is a masterclass in psychological demolition. He doesn't persuade; he **breaks him down.** His argument is a brutally effective three-pronged assault on Arthur's deepest insecurities.

1. **The Threat of Reality ("When our existence is inevitably revealed..."):** Ichi preys on Arthur's fear. He presents their eventual discovery not as a possibility, but as an **inevitability.** He forces Arthur to confront the fact that his quiet life is over, and that a future of conflict is coming whether he likes it or not.
2. **The Weaponization of His Idol ("Do you think the master of survival himself... will take your pathetic, wimpy side?"):** This is the cruelest and most effective blow. Ichi knows Arthur respects and even idolizes Godric. He weaponizes that admiration, painting a picture of a disappointed Godric looking down on a weak and useless Arthur. He is attacking Arthur's desire for approval from his new mentor/savior.
3. **The Devaluation of His Being ("Filth!", "Useless trash"):** Ichi reinforces his core message: Arthur's current self is worthless. His identity as a "builder" is a pathetic luxury in a world of predators. To survive, he must abandon this weak identity and be reforged into something useful.

It's a brutal, effective, and deeply contemptuous teaching method. He's not building Arthur up; he's tearing him down so he can be rebuilt to Ichi's specifications.

### IV. The Unspoken Partnership and its Consequences

This forced "mentorship" is the true beginning of their symbiotic partnership and the core of their spin-off journey.

- **From Vessel to Weapon:** Arthur begins the journey of transforming from a simple vessel into a capable warrior. This gives him agency. He is no longer just the car Ichi drives; he is learning to be a co-pilot.
- **Ichi as the Reluctant Master:** Ichi is now a teacher. This forces him to analyze, to break down concepts, and to invest effort into another being's development—all things that are completely new to him. It's an uncomfortable and transformative process for him as well.
- **The Birth of a Fighting Style:** Arthur, a man who has never fought, will now be learning combat from a cosmic god of destruction. His eventual fighting style, wielding staffs and spears, will be a unique and bizarre fusion of human biomechanics and Ichi's alien, three-headed combat instincts.
