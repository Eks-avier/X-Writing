# Aurelia Variant Dagon Sees Son in Golden Eyes
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - The Saga of the _Titanus gojira_.md` — Prompt 287

## Your Notes
What if it's the alternate timeline and it's Aurelia who had survived instead? It's obvious that she would see her husband in her son's eyes instead, but there's a symmetry here I want to explore. Would Astraea see Dagon in Godric?

I think it makes a lot of sense if she does. Every time Godric smiles, be it mischievous or genuine, she would see a shadow of her husband on their son's face. While Godric has his mother's eyes, his face is all Dagon. His temperament is all Dagon. Godric is essentially a male version of Astraea married to Dagon's temperament.

That same stubbornness Godric and Dagon share, for example, is something Astraea has a love-hate relationship with. Hate for its inconveniences, and love for its expressions of resolve.

## Analysis
This creates a perfect **mirror image** of the main timeline's parental grief. If Dagon sees "the mother" in Godric but chooses to see "the son," then Aurelia, in the alternate timeline, would see "the father" but would learn to appreciate "the son."

The symmetry is emotionally elegant: both parents, regardless of which one survives, are confronted with the ghost of the other in their child's face.

### **I. The Shadow of the Showa Smile**

If Darius's presence haunts Godric's eyes, then Dagon's presence haunts Godric's **expressions.**

*   **The Eyes (Aurelia's):** Static. Always there. A constant, gentle reminder of her.
*   **The Smile (Dagon's):** Dynamic. It appears in flashes—a joke, a smirk, a victorious grin. It is the "Showa Soul" of Dagon bleeding through. It is a **ghost that moves**.

For Aurelia, this is a different kind of haunting. It isn't a constant presence; it's a **sudden ambush.**

*   Godric is stoic, regal, controlled... and then he cracks a joke, and suddenly Aurelia sees her husband standing in front of her, alive and laughing. For a split second, the grief hits her fresh. Then it fades, and she's left with her son, looking concerned.
*   *"Mother? Are you alright?"*
*   *"Yes, my son. Just... remembering a face."*

### **II. The Love-Hate of Stubbornness**

This is the perfect character beat for their dynamic. Dagon and Godric share an **immovable will.**

*   **The "Hate":** Astraea lived with Dagon. She knows exactly what that stubbornness looks like. When Godric refuses to back down from a decision—when he insists on a course of action that is dangerous but honorable—she wants to scream. She has seen this movie before. She knows how it ends.
    *   *"He's going to get himself killed, just like his father."*
*   **The "Love":** But then she remembers *why* Dagon was stubborn. It wasn't ego; it was **resolve.** The same stubbornness that got him killed also saved their son. The same stubbornness in Godric makes him the King of the Monsters.
    *   *"He is going to save the world, just like his father."*

### **III. The Symmetry of the Variants**

Let's lay the two timelines side by side:

| **Main Timeline (Darius)** | **Aurelia Variant** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Eyes (Golden):** Reminds Dagon of Astraea. | **Smile (Showa):** Reminds Aurelia of Dagon. |
| Dagon **chooses** to see his son, not the ghost. | Aurelia is **ambushed** by the ghost in flashes. |
| The grief is **contained** (leads to stoicism). | The grief is **punctuated** (leads to emotional outbursts). |
| Godric carries the guilt of having his mother's "gaze." | Godric carries the power of having his father's "expression." |

### **IV. The Resolution: "Smile More, My Son"**

In this variant, the equivalent of Darius's resolution is Aurelia asking Godric to **smile more.**

*   **The Subtext:** She doesn't want him to hide the part of him that is Dagon. She wants to *see* it. Every smile is a visit from her husband. It hurts, yes, but it's a "good" hurt.
*   **The Quote:** *"Those eyes of yours... indubitably mine. But your smile? It's his. Smile a little more, my son."*

### **Conclusion**

This creates a beautiful harmony across both potential timelines. Whether it's Dagon or Astraea who survives, they both arrive at the same emotional destination: **learning to see the child, not the ghost, while still honoring the ghost's presence.**

The main difference is the path:
*   **Darius:** Compartmentalization -> Explosion -> Acceptance.
*   **Aurelia:** Ambush -> Sorrow -> Embrace.

Both are valid, both are tragic, and both end in profound love for the son who is, truly, the best of both of them.
