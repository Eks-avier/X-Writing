# Darius Grief and Showa Regression
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - The Saga of the _Titanus gojira_.md` — Prompt 243

## Your Notes
The image you painted of Darius is precisely how I envisioned him. However, the truest, freest version of Dagon was during his and Astraea's days.

While Darius remains ever-boisterous in the present era, a piece of him remains somber, muted, and serious. His life has been irrevocably changed, even if his son has won against the circumstances laid against them. Remember, to Dagon, Astraea's death is recent. 10 equivalent years to a human. How many true Earth years would that be? He's been scarred, but now he's lighter, knowing that his son had survived against all odds. So, while Darius *has* regressed back to his true personality, there are quiet moments where the reality of being a widower sets in.

Without the constant danger he "drunk" himself in during the past, the full weight returns to him. His son is alive, after all these years. His son survived... and she didn't. Seeing the happiness of his son evoked the pain of Astraea, who had never really been able to see this. She could never be Aurelia the same way they are Godric and Darius. And now, it's not just his son, it's his grandson Junior, too. He sees two pairs of golden eyes that are hers.

However, I think the conflict lies in his attempt to communicate this to Godric, to prevent the silence that had kept him from truly giving the love their son deserved. Darius is past that now; he needs to let his son know that he loves him.

Darius is still ultimately his Showa-coded figure. He still IS the representative of Showa Godzilla in the ATM AU. However, he has been profoundly scarred, and without the concoction of fear, worry, and grief, he has been reduced to just one potent drink: grief. Darius no longer has to worry about Godric, and all that's left is the gaping hole Astraea left.

## Analysis
This adds a profound and necessary dimension to Darius. It prevents him from becoming a caricature of the "fun dad" and grounds him in the reality of his tragic history.

You are describing a man who is **high-functioning in his grief.** The war is over, the adrenaline has faded, the fear for his son's survival is gone. And when the noise of survival stops, the silence of loss becomes deafening.

This dynamic creates a beautiful, bittersweet texture to his character:

*   **The Showa Exterior:** The boisterous, physical, "Frat King" energy is his authentic self, yes. But it is also a mechanism to keep the silence at bay. He fills the room with noise and life because the quiet reminds him that *she* isn't there to share it.
*   **The Heisei Interior:** Internally, he is living the aftermath of *Godzilla vs. Destoroyah*. He is the survivor walking through the mist, carrying the memory of the fallen.

### **The Double-Edged Gold**

The Golden Eyes of Godric and Junior are the focal point of this conflict.

*   **The Joy:** They are the proof that she existed. They are the beautiful legacy she died to protect.
*   **The Pain:** They are a constant, piercing reminder of exactly what he lost. Every time he looks at his son or grandson, he sees the woman he loved, staring back at him from a world she never got to touch.

This sets up the most important conversation of their lives. Godric needs to know that his father's pain when looking at him isn't regret for *Godric's* existence, but grief for *Astraea's* absence.

### **Scene: The Shadow of the Star**

**(Setting: Late night on the Palace terrace. The "Family Pile" has disbanded. The children are asleep. Godric finds his father sitting alone, a glass of drink in his hand, staring not at the ocean, but at the stars.)**

Godric approached slowly. He knew this mood. It was the same stillness that had terrified him as a hatchling, the back turned against the cold wind. But now, Godric was a man, a King, and he understood the difference between coldness and sorrow.

"You're quiet, Dad," Godric said, leaning on the railing beside him. "The silence doesn't suit you as well as the shouting."

Darius let out a short, rough huff of laughter. "Aye. The shouting is easier, lad." He took a sip, the ice clinking in the glass. "It keeps the ghosts away."

Godric fell silent, letting the moment stretch. He didn't push.

"She would have loved this," Darius whispered, his voice gravelly and thick. He gestured vaguely at the Palace, at the sleeping world below, at the moon hanging heavy in the sky. "The peace. The little ones. She fought so hard for a life she never got to taste."

He turned to look at Godric. In the moonlight, the resemblance was striking, but it was the eyes—even obscured by the blue energy—that held Darius captive.

"I look at you," Darius said, his voice trembling slightly, "and I see her. I look at Junior, and I see her again. Two pairs of those golden eyes, staring back at me."

Godric tightened his grip on the railing. "Does it hurt?"

"Like a knife in the gut," Darius admitted, brutally honest. "Every single time."

Godric looked away, the old shame flickering in his chest. "I'm sorry."

"Nay," Darius's hand shot out, gripping Godric's shoulder with bruising strength. He forced his son to look at him. "Don't you dare apologize. Listen to me, son."

Darius's blue eyes were fierce, burning with the intensity of the Showa hero, but filled with the tears of the Heisei widower.

"It hurts because I miss her. Not because I regret you. For a long time... back in the cave... I couldn't look at you because I was weak. I was afraid that if I saw her in your face, I would break, and I couldn't afford to break. I had to be iron for you."

He softened his grip, his thumb brushing Godric's shoulder.

"But now? I don't have to be iron. I can just be a man who misses his wife." He smiled, a sad, crooked thing. "That pain... it's the price of the ticket, Godric. It's the tax I pay to see her again. Every time I look at you, or that quiet lad of yours, I get to see Astraea live on. And I would pay that tax a thousand times over."

He pulled Godric into a rough, one-armed embrace, leaning his forehead against his son's shoulder.

"You are not my burden," Darius whispered into the night. "You are the only piece of heaven I have left on this earth."
