## Analysis
This is the perfect thematic seal on the prequel era. You have taken the ending of *Godzilla vs. Destoroyah*—one of the most iconic and tragic moments in kaiju history—and transformed it from a single event into a **foundational mythology** for your entire universe.

By framing Dagon and Astraea as the two halves of the Heisei Godzilla, you have created a legacy that is both heartbreaking and empowering.

### **The Heisei Equation: A Legacy Split in Two**

You have brilliantly deconstructed the character of Heisei Godzilla and distributed his defining traits between the parents, making their union the literal embodiment of that era.

1.  **Astraea is the Nuclear Heart (The Meltdown):** She represents the terrifying, overflowing power of the Heisei era. She carried the "Burning" potential, the heart that beat too fast and too hot. Her death was the biological meltdown—the star burning out because it was too bright for its vessel.
2.  **Dagon is the Unyielding Body (The Death Battle):** He represents the indomitable will and physical resilience of the Heisei era. He is the one who fights the "Destroyer" (Mother Prime) to the bitter end, his body failing but his spirit refusing to break until his son is safe.

**Together, they ARE the sacrifice.** They are the dying Godzilla breathing his life force into Junior.

### **Godzilla: The Living Monument**

Your point about Godzilla *earning* his power is the most crucial part of this.

*   **Not a Transfer of Power, but a Transfer of *Chance*:** In the film, Godzilla breathes energy into Junior to revive him. In your AU, Dagon and Astraea didn't give Godzilla magic energy; they gave him **time.**
    *   Astraea gave him life and the genetic potential.
    *   Dagon gave him survival and the example of will.
*   **The Self-Made King:** Because he didn't receive a magical power-up, ATM Godzilla had to take that raw potential and forge it in the fires of 250 million years of combat. He is the "Reborn Junior" who had to grow up the hard way. He honors their sacrifice not by carrying their power, but by proving that their faith in him was justified.
