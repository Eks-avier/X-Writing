# Golden Timeline Family Reunion Desire
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - The Saga of the _Titanus gojira_.md` — Prompt 295

## Your Notes
AHHHHHH, all these conversations make me want a Darius, Aurelia, Godric reunion. None of this will be canon to the main story, of course. But, GOSH, do I want to play with this possibility? I've fallen in love with these three; it's crazy. There's so much chemistry between them, I'm in awe. Even if one is missing or the other, that chemistry still holds. How is this possible? Yet I think there is a sense of tragedy that they would never have this. Makes the readers ***yearn*** for it. I think it feels so real, something you badly want to see but can't.

## Analysis
It is a testament to the strength of your character work that you have created a "Holy Trinity" where the sum is infinitely greater than the parts, yet the parts can never fully assemble.

The chemistry works because they represent a perfect, self-correcting emotional circuit:
*   **Darius** provides the **Energy** (Joy/Action).
*   **Aurelia** provides the **Center** (Grace/Soul).
*   **Godric** provides the **Structure** (Duty/Strength).

Without one, the others have to overcompensate. But together? They are unstoppable.

You are right; the fact that this full reunion is impossible in the main canon is what gives the story its haunting beauty. It is the **"Phantom Limb"** of the narrative—the reader can feel the presence of the missing parent in every scene, ache for it, and understand exactly what is lost. That yearning is what makes Godric's relationship with his own children so precious; he is building the table he never got to sit at.

**However...** we are writers. We can indulge in the "What If." Let's treat ourselves to a glimpse of that **Golden Timeline.** Let's see what a Sunday afternoon at the Nordson Palace would look like if the universe had been kind.

### **The Impossible Sunday: The Golden Timeline**

**(Setting: The Alpha Wing Kitchen. It is chaotic. It is loud. It is perfect.)**

"You are burning it, husband."

Aurelia didn't look up from her book, her voice cool and melodious, cutting through the sizzling noise of the stove. She was perched on a stool, wearing a dark silk robe that made her look like a presiding judge, sipping tea with an elegance that defied the chaos around her.

"Nonsense, woman!" Darius roared, flipping a slab of meat that was definitely charred on one side. He was shirtless, wearing an apron that said *KING OF THE GRILL* (a gift from Leo, undoubtedly). "It's 'Northern Char!' It builds character! Godric loves it, don't you, son?"

Godric, leaning against the counter with a mug of coffee, looked between his parents. He wore a simple t-shirt, his posture relaxed, his guard completely down. He looked at the charred meat. He looked at his mother's raised eyebrow.

"I plead the Fifth," Godric deadpanned.

"Coward," Aurelia murmured, hiding a smile behind her teacup. "You get that from your father."

"I am the bravest man alive!" Darius protested, pointing a spatula at her. "I married you, didn't I?"

Godric choked on his coffee. Aurelia slowly lowered her cup. Her golden eyes—identical to Godric's—fixed on her husband with a predatory gleam that would have terrified a lesser Titan.

"Is that a complaint, Darius?"

"A boast, my love! A boast of my supreme fortitude!" Darius backpedaled with the grace of a man who had survived 500 years of marriage. He winked at Godric. "See? She keeps me sharp. If it weren't for her, I'd have grown soft and fat like a seal."

"If it weren't for me," Aurelia countered, standing up and gliding over to the stove, "you would be eating ash." She gently took the spatula from his hand. "Go sit down. You are stressing the meat."

Darius grinned, surrendering instantly. He walked over to Godric, throwing a heavy arm around his son's shoulders. They stood there, two generations of Kings, watching the Queen salvage lunch.

"She's terrifying," Darius whispered loudly.

"Terrifying," Godric agreed, his voice warm.

Aurelia glanced back over her shoulder. Her gaze softened as she looked at them—the white-haired, boisterous warrior and the dark-haired, stoic king. Her boys.

"I can hear you," she said, but there was no bite in it. "Set the table. The twins will be back from their flight with Rodan soon, and if there isn't food, Leo will eat the furniture."

"Aye, aye, Captain," Darius saluted. He squeezed Godric's shoulder. "You heard your mother, lad. To the table."

Godric watched them. The bickering, the touch, the easy rhythm of a life lived together. For a second, the image shimmered, threatened by the reality of a cold cave and a lonely death... but here, in this impossible moment, the image held.

He smiled—a true, unburdened smile that reached his golden eyes.

"Right behind you, Dad."

### **Why This Hurts (In a Good Way)**

*   **The Banter:** You see exactly how Aurelia tempers Darius. She reins in his chaos not by stopping it, but by steering it.
*   **The "Coward" Joke:** Godric being afraid to pick a side between his parents is a dynamic he *never got to have.* It's the luxury of a child with two living parents.
*   **The Visuals:** The contrast of Darius's "Frat Dad" energy and Aurelia's "Gothic Queen" energy is visually hilarious and charming.
*   **The Harmony:** It shows that Godric isn't an anomaly; he is the perfect blend of these two people.

This vignette serves a purpose. It establishes the **emotional baseline** of the family. Even if it never happens in the main timeline, knowing that *this* is the chemistry they share makes every interaction between Godric and Darius, or Godric and the memory of Aurelia, deeper. You know what they are missing. You know the shape of the hole in their hearts.
