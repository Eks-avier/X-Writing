# Godric Sees Aurelia in His Eyes
> **Source:** `Formatted_With_Thoughts - The Saga of the _Titanus gojira_.md` — Prompt 286

## Your Notes
Godric has been "thinned" by Aurelia, who also possessed a more slender frame before her death. This reflects in Godric's slender but powerful figure beneath all of his bulk. Unlike Junior, you'd have to see Godric as his Titan form to see this. This is also why despite possessing Dagon's eyes, Godric sometimes sees his mother in the mirror than himself. And that's sad. Honestly, it's ***his*** eyes.

But Darius does not see Astraea in Godric's eyes, he sees his son. He's not haunted by his wife every time he looks at his son. The tragedy of Astraea, while deeply wounding him, didn't scar him enough to bleed every time he looks at their child. Dagon actually manages it well, having decided during Godric's first cry to see his child first and not be blinded by grief. It would take 10 `gojira` years for Dagon to even feel the scar of his wife's death on his psyche, which is why the grief only showed up after his long migration. This was probably a bad decision, but it's one Dagon can live with.

## Analysis
This creates a devastating **asymmetry of grief**. The father successfully detaches his child from his wife's death, while the son **internalizes** the connection and lets it haunt him for eons.

It is a masterclass in how trauma can be processed differently across generations, and how the "better" choice of one generation can inadvertently cause pain for the next.

### **I. The Son Who Sees the Ghost**

Godric's relationship with his reflection is a narrative goldmine.

*   **The Eyes:** Dagon's eyes (shape) + Astraea's eyes (color). They are a fusion, but Godric only sees the **Aurelia** in them. He ignores the aspects of his father.
*   **The "Inherited Guilt":** Godric believes, on some deep, unspoken level, that his mother died because of him. Her golden eyes stare back from his face and silently accuse him: *"You lived because I didn't."*
*   **The Avoidance:** This is why he avoids mirrors and looking at his own human eyes. He doesn't see himself; he sees the mother he "replaced." It's not vanity; it's guilt.

### **II. The Father Who Made a Choice**

Dagon's psychological decision at the moment of Godric's first cry is both noble and tragic.

*   **The Decision:** He made a conscious, willful choice: **"This is my son. He is not his mother's ghost."** He saw the golden eyes and, through sheer Krator will, refused to let them represent loss. He forced them to represent hope.
*   **The Cost:** By "bottling" his grief for 10 Gojira years, he allowed it to ferment into something darker. When the migration was over and the immediate danger passed, the grief hit him like a collapsing star. It was the "delayed bomb" that made him cold.
*   **The Irony:** His choice to protect his son from being a symbol of grief inadvertently taught the son that his mother's eyes were something to be hidden from. Dagon didn't pass on his grief, but he passed on the **silence**. And Godric filled that silence with his own interpretation: "He doesn't talk about her because looking at me hurts him."

### **III. The Resolution: Darius and Junior**

In the modern era, this is resolved through the generational filter.

*   **Darius's View:** When Darius (Dagon reborn) looks at Godric, he doesn't just see a man with Astraea's eyes. He sees a 250-million-year-old Krator King. The eyes are no longer "haunted"; they are the proof that Astraea's sacrifice *worked*. He is proud of them.
*   **Junior's Role:** Junior has the same golden eyes. But Godric doesn't see "Aurelia" in Junior's eyes; he sees his **son**. This is the moment Godric realizes he was doing to himself what Dagon consciously chose *not* to do. If he can look at Junior and feel only love, why can't he look at himself and feel the same?

### **IV. The Tableau of Mirrors**

Imagine this scene:

The Nordson men stand before a mirror.
*   **Darius:** Smiles. He sees his son's legacy and his wife's hope.
*   **Godric:** Looks away. He sees his mother's ghost.
*   **Junior:** Meets his father's eyes in the reflection. He sees nothing but family.

And it is Junior's lack of trauma that finally allows Godric to see that the eyes were never a curse. They were a **gift**.

### **Conclusion**

This detail adds a heartbreaking layer to Godric's otherwise stoic exterior. He isn't just a King with burdens; he is a man who has spent eons believing his own face is a memorial to his mother's death. Darius's presence, and especially Junior's innocent gaze, is what finally allows him to see **himself** in the mirror.
