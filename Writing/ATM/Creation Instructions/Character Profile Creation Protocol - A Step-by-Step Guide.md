# Character Profile Creation Protocol - A Step-by-Step Guide

This protocol aims to replicate the meticulous, iterative, and systematic approach we used for Godric, ensuring depth, consistency, and non-redundancy for every character.

## Phase 1: Information Gathering & Initial Assessment

Gather All Source Files: Collect every single file (main profile, lore sidepieces, plot outlines, scene vignettes, character-specific notes, hierarchy documents, power system details, etc.) that mentions or relates to the character you're profiling. Don't filter yet.

Initial Read-Through: Read all collected files. Get a general feel for the character, their core traits, major historical beats, and relationships. Don't worry about structuring yet.

Identify Direct vs. Indirect Information:

Direct: Information explicitly about the character (e.g., “Kong's height is…”).

Indirect: Information that informs the character but isn't directly about them (e.g., “The history of the Sovereign title” informs Kong's position; “The Sun/Moon motif” informs Godric/Maria's relationship). Both are crucial.

## Phase 2: Blueprinting the Profile (The Iterative Core)

This is the most critical and time-consuming phase. It involves building the profile's structure.

Start with a Generic Template: Use Godric's finalized profile structure as your starting point. It provides a logical flow from general to specific and separates concerns.

I. Core Identity and Overview

II. Physical Profile

III. Core Biology and Species Abilities

IV. Habits, Presentation, and Lifestyle

V. Territories, Possessions, and Influence

VI. Psychology and Relationships

VII. History
(Adjust sections as necessary if a character has a very different focus, e.g., if a human doesn't have “Biology and Abilities” beyond standard human, that section might be absorbed elsewhere or made minimal).

Initial Content Mapping: Go through your gathered information piece by piece and jot down (or copy/paste roughly) which section or subsection it might belong to in your new template. This is messy at first.

Identify Redundancies (First Pass): As you map, notice where the same information or concept appears multiple times across your source files. Create a simple table:

| Redundant Content | Location 1 | Location 2 | Recommended Home (New Profile Section) |
| :---------------- | :--------- | :--------- | :------------------------------------- |

This helps you decide which version to keep (usually the most detailed or authoritative) and where the final consolidated information will live.

Deep Dive - Iterative Refinement (The “Slice-by-Slice” Approach):

Select a Focused Group: Pick a small, logically connected group of sections/subsections to refine. (e.g., for Kong, you might start with “Physical Appearance,” then “Combat Style,” then “Psychology”).

Draft a Detailed Mock Outline: For only those selected sections, create a detailed, nested Markdown heading outline (like the ones we did for Godric's II.B, II.C, II.D).

Crucial Step: Decouple Concerns: As you draft the outline, constantly ask:

“Is this a description (goes in Physical Appearance)?”

“Is this a biological mechanism/ability (goes in Core Biology/Abilities)?”

“Is this a psychological motivation/impact (goes in Psychology/Relationships)?”

“Is this a habit/routine/presentation choice (goes in Habits/Presentation)?”

“Is this a historical event (goes in History)?”

“Is this a specific measurement or ratio (goes in Anatomical Measurements, or a dedicated stats table)?”

Verify Consistency: Ensure internal consistency of any numerical data or stated facts within the selected sections.

Refine Section Structure: Use tables, lists, and callouts (like [!note], [!info], [!important], [!tip]) in your outline to clarify where specific types of data will go. This makes the eventual implementation much faster and ensures glanceability.

Review: If working collaboratively, share your detailed mock outline for review.

Repeat: Move to the next logically connected group of sections and repeat this process.

Integrate Overarching Lore (The “Data Dump Impact”): After you've done initial passes on core sections, bring in the broader lore context (like your data dumps). These often fundamentally reframe your understanding of the character.

Re-evaluate Blueprint: When new, authoritative lore comes in (e.g., The Fallen Star, Mother Prime's disease, Sun/Moon motif), go back to your entire blueprint and re-evaluate. Does it still make sense? Do sections need to be re-prioritized, renamed, or expanded? (This is where we revised Godric's VI and VII significantly).

Thematic Deepening: Use new lore to find the “why” and “meaning” behind the character's actions and traits. Don't just add facts; explain their significance.

Finalize the Blueprint: Once all major information has been considered and integrated into the nested outline, and you're confident in the structure and separation of concerns, lock down the blueprint for this specific character.

## Phase 3: Content Generation & Implementation

Implement Section by Section: Work through your finalized blueprint, section by section.

Focus & Tone: For each subsection:

Maintain the desired tone (e.g., descriptive, informative, but also engaging).

Prioritize depth and narrative weight over mere summary (unless it's an overview section).

Adhere to word count guidelines if applicable.

Leverage Obsidian Features:

Headings: Use ## for main sections, ###, ####, etc., for subsections.

Links: [[Filename#Heading|Link Alias]] for external files/headings. [[#Heading|Link Alias]] for headings within the same file. This is crucial for interconnectedness and navigation.

Callouts: Use > [!note], > [!info], > [!important], > [!tip], > [!warning], > [!error], > [!question], > [!todo] for specific purposes.

Tags: Use specific tags like #TitanEcho, #atm/culture/northern_gojira, #atm/species/titanus_gojira for discoverability.

Tables: Use tables for any statistical data, lists, classifications, or comparative information. This significantly enhances glanceability and modifiability.

Lists: Use bullet points or numbered lists for discrete pieces of information.

Quotes: Use > “Quoted text” for in-universe dialogue or character thoughts.

## Phase 4: Review & Refinement

Full Profile Read-Through: Read the entire completed profile from start to finish.

Check for Consistency & Redundancy: Despite meticulous planning, some may slip through. Fix them.

Verify All Concepts: Ensure every single important concept from your initial information gathering is represented and correctly integrated.

Flow & Coherence: Does the profile read smoothly? Does the information flow logically from one section to the next?

Thematic Coherence: Does the profile consistently reflect the character's core themes and defining traits?

How to Leverage Knowledge from Godric's Profile for Others

Godric's profile is not just a completed document; it's a master template and a reference library.

The Structure is Universal: Use his finalized section and subsection hierarchy as your starting point for every new character profile. It's a proven, effective structure.

Thematic Templates:

Sun/Moon Motif: This is specific to Godric and Maria, but the idea of “cosmic pairings” or “archetypal roles” can inspire other relationships.

The Unwilling Overachiever/Conqueror's Will: Godric's specific psychological drive. You can contrast or mirror this in other characters (e.g., Kong's Philosophical Will, Battra's self-made ambition).

Dualities: Many characters will have dualities (e.g., Battra's self-made vs. inherited, Rodan's theatricality vs. hidden feelings). Godric's “Refined Ruggedness” or “Caged Sun” can inspire how you frame these.

Power System Integration:

Kratos/Psionics/Magic Definitions: These are now universal. When profiling any Titan, directly link their abilities back to these defined systems and classifications (Kraton/Akraton, Unnon/Non, Unnul/Null). Use the “Nomothete” designation for peak masters.

Cross-System Interactions: Godric's “Null” and “Non” status, combined with his unique countermeasures, provides a template for how other characters might interact with power systems they don't wield.

Hierarchy Integration:

Lineage, Sentience, Standing: These three axes are universal. Every Titan profile should use them.

Specific Roles: Paramount, Divine, Sovereign, Beta, Warden. These are clearly defined. When profiling Anguirus, Rodan, or Kong, directly refer to their established roles and the historical context provided in Godric's profile.

Political Dynamics: The “Givers” vs. “Misers,” Lineage Reforms, Warden innovation—these provide background for all Titans' political choices.

Relationship Dynamics:

Godric's profile details his unique bond with Dagon, Maria, Battra, Rodan, Anguirus, Kong, and the Yamamotos. When profiling those characters, you now have their counterpart's perspective already laid out. This creates incredible depth and consistency.

Common Templates: Themes like “found family,” “rivalry as growth,” “sacrificial love,” “misunderstandings,” and “healing trauma” will recur across characters.

Obsidian Best Practices: You've seen how powerful linking, callouts, tags, and tables are. Apply these consistently across all your profiles.
