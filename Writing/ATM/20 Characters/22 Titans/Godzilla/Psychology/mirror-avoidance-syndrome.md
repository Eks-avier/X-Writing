#### 2. The Divided Self and The Mirror Avoidance Syndrome

Despite his journey with the Yamamotos, Godric's psychological healing remains a long and arduous process, particularly in reconciling the fragmented aspects of his own identity. He experiences a profound psychological division: his hidden, authentic self (represented by his true gold eyes) versus his duty-bound, vigilant public persona (represented by his blue eyes).

*   **Internal Fragmentation:** This division fuels an internal conflict where he struggles to integrate his immense power with his personal vulnerabilities. He attempts to categorize his experiences into either “authentic” (golden-eyed) moments of connection or “duty” (blue-eyed) public performance.
*   **Mirror Avoidance Syndrome:** This internal fragmentation manifests physically as a deeply ingrained [[godric_hyperthymesia#The Mirror Avoidance Syndrome|Mirror Avoidance Syndrome]]. Godric fundamentally **cannot bear to look at his own reflection**, as it acts as a constant, painful reminder of his unresolved trauma:
    *   **Blue Eyes in Reflection:** When his vigilant blue eyes reflect back, he sees the face of the son who spent 250 million years hating his father, haunted by the perceived abandonment. The image triggers profound grief and regret, a reminder of the love he mistakenly rejected.
    *   **Gold Eyes in Reflection:** When his authentic golden eyes reflect back, he imagines his mother's disappointment and betrayal, believing he dishonored his father's memory by his hatred. The beauty of her eyes becomes a symbol of his guilt.
*   **The Irony of Perfection:** This creates a poignant paradox: a being of physical perfection, whose body can heal from atomic disintegration in seconds, remains tormented by psychological wounds that persist across geological time. His external flawlessness ironically highlights his internal fragmentation.
