# The One-Shot Doctrine

> *"If it can be done in one shot, do it."*

The One-Shot Doctrine is the first principle of gojira combat philosophy—a cultural expression of supreme confidence and ruthless efficiency.

---

## Core Principle

Combat is a **problem to be solved**. The most elegant solution is the quickest. If an opponent can be eliminated in a single decisive action, that action should be taken immediately.

This is not cruelty—it is **respect**:
- For the opponent's potential threat
- For the efficiency of conflict resolution
- For the gojira's own energy reserves

Prolonging a fight that could end instantly is considered wasteful.

---

## Cultural Context

### Status Through Efficiency
Within gojira culture, one-shotting an opponent is the **ultimate sign of respect**. It says: "I took you seriously enough to end this immediately."

Conversely, toying with an opponent—when immediate victory was possible—is seen as:
- Wasteful of energy
- Disrespectful of the opponent's danger
- Arrogant rather than confident

### Competitive Application
Inter-factional competitions among gojira often center on finding **creative one-shot methods**. The goal is not merely to win but to win *elegantly*—to demonstrate such complete understanding of combat that victory can be achieved in a single action.

---

## Finisher-Heavy Arsenal

The One-Shot Doctrine explains why gojira combat techniques are predominantly **finishers**:

| Technique | One-Shot Potential |
|-----------|-------------------|
| [[atomic-breath/CLAUDE\|Atomic Breath]] | Primary ranged finisher |
| [[death-roll\|Death Roll]] | Grappling finisher |
| [[nuclear-pulse/CLAUDE\|Nuclear Pulse]] | Multi-target finisher |
| Enhanced strikes | Close-combat finishers |

Every tool in the gojira arsenal is designed to **end fights**, not to score points or create openings. The opening is the ending.

---

## Interaction with Attrition

The One-Shot Doctrine does not contradict the [[attrition-doctrine|Attrition Doctrine]]—they operate on different timescales:

| Doctrine | Timescale | Application |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **One-Shot** | Momentary | When decisive action is possible |
| **Attrition** | Extended | When decisive action is not immediately possible |

A gojira attempts the one-shot first. If it fails, they shift to attrition. The doctrines are sequential, not opposing.

---

## Thematic Significance

The One-Shot Doctrine reflects the gojira self-image as **apex predators**:

- Predators don't "fight"—they dispatch prey
- Prolonged conflict means miscalculation
- Victory should be certain before engagement begins

This philosophy shapes everything about gojira combat: the arsenal, the mindset, the cultural values.
