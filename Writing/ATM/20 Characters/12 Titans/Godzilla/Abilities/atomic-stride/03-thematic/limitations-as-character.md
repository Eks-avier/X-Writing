# Limitations as Character

> "It's amusing that the Stride is defined by its flaws more than its usefulness. That's intentional."

---

## The Design Philosophy

The Atomic Stride is a technique **defined by what it can't do** as much as what it can. This is deliberate characterization through mechanics.

## Why Flaws Matter

Most power systems define abilities by their capabilities. The Stride inverts this:

| Standard Approach | Stride Approach |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| "This ability lets you..." | "This ability *would* let you, but..." |
| Focus on power ceiling | Focus on practical constraints |
| Limitations as afterthought | Limitations as identity |

## Character Through Constraint

The Stride's flaws reveal Godric's character:

### He's Not a Magic User
The Stride has no "auto-aim." He must calculate trajectory, distance, and power output manually. Every use is a feat of high-speed mental processing—physics, not magic.

### He's Still Learning
The technique required development, training, and Maria's help. Even the Alpha Paramount must work to grow.

### Power Has Costs
Shockwaves, radiation, collateral damage—his abilities have consequences he must actively manage rather than ignore.

### Control Is Earned
The Mantle wasn't a power boost but a *control mechanism*. Growth came through restraint, not escalation.

## Narrative Function

A technique without flaws is boring. A technique whose flaws drive story development, require relationships to solve, and reveal character through constraint—that's compelling.

The Stride needed Maria's training barriers. Needed Kong's philosophy. Needed the Mantle to become practical. Each limitation created a narrative opportunity.
