      * Sukuna's Shrine's Cleave and Dismantle literally reflect his hedonism, and might-as-meaning philosophy.
* What doesn't exist in Krátos is a universal tier of progression (Sealed to Shikai to Bankai or Haki to Advanced Haki).
    
   * JJK's Cursed Techniques also share this distinction with Krátos. Gojo's Lapse Blue, Reversal Red, and Hollow Purple are unique to him and only him, just as Fushiguro's Mahoraga and Divine Dogs are to him, and Sukuna/Itadori's Cleave and Dismantle are to theirs.
     * In fact, this distinction is so strong in the series that Itadori Yuuji's Shrine and Sukuna's Shrine are the same CT, yet with completely *different* manifestations!
         * This principle is the same as in Krátos.
            * Dagon and Godzilla are father and son; both belong in the same Northern Faction; both possess the exact same classification, yet the manner in which they wield their power is wholly unique to them.
               * The same can be said for Junior and Leo, who already possess a more distinct form of usage just from construct creation alone!
   * Despite the similarities with JJK, the Magic power system of the ATM AU is actually supposed to reference it outright! Krátos, having parallels with it, was just a happy coincidence.
* The limitations in Krátos serve not as a barrier, but as a hurdle to conquer (pun intended); it is progression despite limitation.
   * Zuko learning lightning-redirection is actually an example of this 'growth through limitation.' He can't learn lightning-bending, but he can counter it.
   * JJK's evolution in its system is also a disciple of this principle. Techniques cannot be changed, nor can they be given, even willingly, but the creativity and innovation of a sorcerer factor heavily into this.
     * Surprise, surprise, this is also a facet in the Magic power system of the ATM AU. It is, in fact, a unifying theme among all three systems.
   * Godzilla learning Atomic Amplification as a response to his transformation into a human is a concrete example of this.
      * He cannot use his Atomic Breath in a human body without hurting himself.
         * So, he abandons it in favor of CQC.
         * Meanwhile, he and Kong develop the preliminary concept of Atomic Amplification.
      * It runs counteractive to his personal philosophy, so it takes a while to settle in.
      * Eventually, he masters it so completely that he teaches Atomic Amplification to his father and sons.
         * And *even then*, everything about their rendition of Atomic Amplification is unique to their character. Although I admit this is easier to portray in an audio-visual medium than in a textual one.
* Progression exists in Krátos, but it's subtle and specific to the species, lineage, and individual wielding it.

But, we're overstating how the 98% submission rate interacts with Psychics and Sorcerers. The submission rate within Krátos is absolute, but only within the system. It is granular and must interact with the rules of other systems; otherwise, that is not to say they are immune to it—they have tools to counteract it, and even that is not guaranteed. Just like Krátos has its own, so too do the other systems, and they can't be bypassed up front any more than the other two systems can bypass Krátos's rules.

For Koinon, a Conqueror's presence is instant and K.O. if they don't possess a baseline level of will to withstand it. But, for Psychics, the best of whom are often trained with mental guards, and Sorcerers, the best of whom are often trained with many mental protection spells, their performance against a Conqueror depends entirely on their skill and how well their specialties match up against their opponent. However, that does not mean psychics and sorcerers cannot be affected by a Conqueror's Aura. They still suspect its effects if they have nothing to answer for it.

The Conqueror is a personification of willpower in the ATM Universe. Instead of framing them as hurdles to character agency, framing them as magnifiers of other characters is better. Humans like Madison Russell and her father, Mark Russell, could stand to be beside Godzilla daily. It speaks to their willpower that they can do so, not simply because Madison and Mark are prominent human characters in the AU, and thus they should have the privilege of interacting with one of the main characters leading the Titan-perspective narratives. This principle applies to Magic and Psionics. Battra is the pinnacle of Magic, and thus the avatar of knowledge. His students' managing to understand him and internalize his lessons speaks to their capacity to learn and strategize. Mothra is the strongest psychic on Earth. She's also the most beautiful and most empathic of all Titans. Wherever she goes, life blooms and follows her.

Further, direct confrontation is NEVER the intention against Conquerors. Psychics and Sorcerers actually have an advantage against Krátons because it's never within their system's framework to attack head-on anyway; that's Krátos's specialty. It's also why, within Krátos, Koinon often fails against Krator. No normal sorcerer, psychic, or Kráton should have the right to contest against someone like Godzilla of all beings. He sits at the top due to his strength of character; his place among the power systems just becomes an additional indicator of that notion. Yet, it is also entirely possible for Godzilla to be outmaneuvered if said sorcerer manages to play to the highest strengths of his system. The ball then passes to Godzilla's court, and readers will get to see how he responds. Each system can go back and forth and converse with the others.

Conquerors are stat-checks against other systems' wielders' willpower and self-authenticity. Among many, one of the main reasons why Battra loses against Godzilla in their fight is that he tries to overpower him rather than lean into his magic's strengths, which are trickery, deceit, and strategy. This response is motivated by what he feels against Godzilla, which is downstream from what he feels against his twin sister. Yet, he manages to give Godzilla one of the best fights in his life anyway because Battra's personal willpower to succeed, along with his strategy and mastery of magic, is enough to “clash” against Godzilla's own qualities. It's why I presented their fight as a perfect opportunity to show the balance between each system. BOTH Godzilla and Battra demonstrate all three systems' strengths, despite Godzilla being only natively Kráton while Battra being both Psychic and Sorcerer. Their fight is just as much a conversation of their capabilities as it is a spectacle to behold.

Each system has a requirement in combat:

Fighting against sorcerers requires mechanical skill and knowledge.

Fighting against psychics requires mental stability and emotional intelligence

Fighting against krátons requires willpower.

Such requirements are derived from the fundamental philosophy behind each power system:

Krátos: Ontological

Magic: Epistemological

Psionics: Aesthetical

Regardless of the system, each character must possess a certain level of quality represented, and thus demanded, by the system. A Telepath needs skill and knowledge against a Flame Sorcerer and strong willpower against a Pyroton, on top of their personal psionic powers. Each system tests a character's specific stat, regardless of whether they are native to a particular system or don't belong to ANY system. There is even more depth to this. Someone who is a Telepath may require a strategy against an Ice Sorcerer as demanded by the Philosophy of Magic, yet the former may also want to have superior determination to succeed against the latter. Even though neither telepath nor sorcerer is Krátons, the telepath's will to succeed in the fight can tip the scales to their favor. Borrowing the philosophies of other systems is just as valid as sticking to the native philosophy of one's native system.

Circling back to Avatar, the Last Airbender's bending system, this is much like what Iroh had told Zuko about the harmony between the four elements, which led to the former's creation of Lightning Redirection; it was a skill inspired by waterbending principles. Similarly, while there are definite boundaries between the systems in the ATM Universe, the best of the best often frame these barriers as gateways to wells of inspiration. Understanding the fundamental philosophies of each system is an indication of character growth or innate excellence, or even both. Kong is the GREATEST example of this principle. He is an Akraton, Null, and Non, meaning he has no place in any power system. Yet, he garners attention from the likes of beings such as Godzilla, Ghidorah, Mothra, and Battra, despite all four's capability to annihilate him with a sneeze. Why? Because he carries the qualities all three systems represent. Even if he has hard limits and encounters them faster than his cosmic peers, he tries his best to overcome them anyway (Krátos) with his skill, strategy, and tactics (Magic), and composure in times of crisis (Psionic). Krátos is just one piece of a three-way puzzle that forms the reality of the Antitheriomorphosis Universe.
